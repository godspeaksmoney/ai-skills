# Research synthesis

This file turns the Research archive into analytical methods you can use. Each method is an original paraphrase, with the papers that support it given in braces as `[ID]` links to the study notes. The archive gives the methods. The weights, grades and scenario probabilities in this skill's scoring rubric are choices made by the skill or the analyst; they are not Mauboussin's.

## How the methods fit together

A stock's price is a forecast of future cash flows, so the task is to compare what the price implies with what the business can plausibly deliver [038](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-038) [104](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-104) [152](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-152). Mauboussin's work keeps coming back to one chain of reasoning: what customers value, and the competitive position, set the returns on incremental investment. Returns and reinvestment drive cash flow. Competition makes excess returns fade. The price implies a specific path for all of this, and the question is whether that path is too optimistic or too pessimistic. Base rates discipline each link, and decision hygiene keeps the analyst honest [064](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-064) [103](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-103) [010](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-010).

## 1. Competitive advantage and fade

- **Two questions: how big the moat is, and how long it lasts.** Size it with the spread between ROIC and the cost of capital. Judge how long it lasts from industry structure, firm-specific added value and stability [143](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-143) [101](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-101) [062](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-062) [012](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-012). Firm effects explain more of the differences in performance than industry effects, but industry still shapes how long top performers stay on top [062](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-062) [143](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-143).
- **The competitive advantage period (CAP).** Value depends on how long excess returns persist. The market-implied CAP is the expectation to judge, and it can lengthen or shorten without any change in earnings [180](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-180) [004](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-004) [172](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-172).
- **Fade is the default.** ROIC and growth regress toward the mean, at speeds that differ by sector. Model the fade explicitly: expected value = mean + correlation × (current − mean) [100](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-100) [116](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-116) [022](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-022) [097](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-097) [114](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-114). Top performers rarely stay on top, and sustained turnarounds are uncommon [116](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-116) [138](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-138).
- **Market share stability is a practical durability test.** Low five-year share change signals a stable industry. Concentration ratios are poor guides to advantage [029](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-029) [012](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-012).
- **Life cycle.** A company's stage (introduction, growth, maturity, shake-out, decline) sets reasonable expectations and capital priorities [020](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-020) [011](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-011) [031](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-031) [153](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-153).

## 2. Network effects, increasing returns and market power

- Increasing returns come from supply-side scale, learning curves, demand-side network effects and intangibles. Judge each source's durability separately [017](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-017) [059](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-059) [134](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-134).
- Network markets tend to tip once they reach critical mass, at modest penetration. Metcalfe-style valuation rules overstate the value of large networks [134](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-134) [160](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-160) [172](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-172). Winner-take-most outcomes show up as power-law distributions in traffic and value [162](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-162) [159](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-159).
- Ecosystems and keystone positions can create advantages beyond a single product [128](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-128) [155](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-155). Markups and apparent market power shrink once intangible costs are counted properly [029](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-029) [017](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-017).
- For platforms and exchanges, liquidity begets liquidity. Anything that pools liquidity across venues weakens a venue-specific network effect. *(Applied in the IEX example.)*

## 3. Profit pools and industry analysis

- Map the industry and its profit pool before analysing the firm. Value creation is unevenly distributed and migrates over time [066](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-066) [143](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-143) [062](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-062). Strong industries contain value destroyers and weak ones contain creators [062](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-062) [012](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-012).
- Combine the industry map with five forces, threat of entry, customer willingness to pay and disruption risk [012](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-012) [165](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-165) [167](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-167).

## 4. Customer economics

- Value equals the customer lifetime value of current customers plus that of future customers, net of acquisition cost. Model retention by cohort and carry everything through to free cash flow, including taxes and investment [040](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-040) [133](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-133).
- Small gains in retention raise customer value disproportionately. Constant-retention shortcuts can misstate cohort value [040](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-040) [133](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-133) [155](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-155) [167](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-167).
- Estimate total addressable market by triangulating bottom-up conversion, diffusion models and base rates. Diffusion models miss replacement cycles, scale and network effects [078](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-078) [150](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-150).

## 5. ROIC and incremental returns

- Calculate ROIC consistently: subtract excess cash, show results with and without goodwill, and split ROIC into margin and turnover [028](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-028) [091](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-091).
- Growth adds value only when the return on incremental capital exceeds the cost of capital. When it falls short, growth destroys value [045](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-045) [095](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-095) [132](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-132) [174](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-174).
- Returns follow changes in ROIC relative to expectations, not the level of ROIC [022](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-022) [097](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-097) [114](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-114). The market rewards improvement and punishes deterioration [097](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-097).
- ROIIC is a noisy indicator, not a true rate of return. Market-expected return on investment (MEROI) gives a price-implied benchmark [041](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-041) [151](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-151) [182](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-182).

## 6. Intangibles

- Expensed intangible investment depresses reported earnings and invested capital. Reclassify part of SG&A and R&D using company-specific splits and asset lives [033](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-033) [043](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-043) [027](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-027) [036](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-036).
- After adjustment, ROIC distributions narrow and some apparent superstars look less exceptional [027](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-027) [017](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-017). Intangible-heavy firms grow faster but with wider dispersion, so refine base rates for them [039](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-039).
- Distinguish accounting losses from economic losses: firms with GAAP losses caused by investment can be good businesses [030](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-030) [038](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-038).

## 7. Maintenance versus growth investment

- Only spending above maintenance funds growth. Maintenance capex exceeds depreciation by roughly a fifth in aggregate, and intangible maintenance spending matters as well [035](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-035) [026](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-026).
- Negative free cash flow is fine when incremental returns exceed the cost of capital. Track whether revisions to earnings and sales keep pace with capex [182](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-182) [030](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-030).

## 8. Capital allocation and governance

- The capital-allocation series supplies base rates. Internal financing dominates. Rapid asset growth predicts weak returns. Most M&A value goes to sellers. Cash deals beat stock deals. Spinoffs and divestitures tend to add value [090](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-090) [081](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-081) [063](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-063) [026](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-026) [007](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-007). Patterns outside the U.S. differ by region [087](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-087) [061](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-061) [074](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-074).
- M&A tests: shareholder value at risk, synergy value versus premium, the signal sent by the financing choice, and the strategic category [057](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-057) [176](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-176) [112](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-112) [110](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-110).
- Buybacks create value only below intrinsic value and when no better use of cash exists. EPS accretion proves nothing. Buybacks and issuance transfer wealth between groups of holders [092](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-092) [105](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-105) [125](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-125) [175](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-175) [069](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-069) [032](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-032) [013](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-013).
- Count stock-based compensation once, as an expense or as dilution, and treat outstanding options as claims [023](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-023) [135](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-135) [173](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-173). Benchmark excess cash against intangible intensity and governance [008](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-008). Incentives shape behaviour, so reward process rather than luck [108](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-108).

## 9. Valuation and the reverse DCF

- Everything that generates cash is a DCF problem. Multiples compress a DCF and hide its assumptions [038](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-038) [015](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-015) [051](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-051) [095](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-095).
- Split value into steady-state value plus the present value of growth opportunities (PVGO). The PVGO share is a gauge of expectations [002](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-002) [040](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-040) [095](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-095).
- Reverse-engineer the price: solve for the growth, margin, CAP or MEROI it implies, then judge plausibility against base rates [104](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-104) [180](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-180) [004](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-004) [041](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-041) [154](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-154) [157](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-157).
- Common DCF errors include explicit forecasts that are too short, terminal values inconsistent with returns and growth, and inconsistent discount rates [123](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-123) [177](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-177).
- Cost of capital: use a ten-year government yield, a forward-looking equity risk premium, beta adjusted toward one, and market-value weights [025](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-025) [099](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-099). Low rates and high volatility raise the value of real options, but such options matter for few firms [042](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-042) [166](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-166).
- Total shareholder return decomposes into earnings growth, change in the multiple, dividend yield and share count. Use the TSR checklist [019](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-019).

## 10. Base rates and Bayesian updating

- Start from the outside view (a reference class), then adjust with case evidence. The weight on the base rate rises with the role of luck and with the forecast horizon [064](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-064) [103](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-103) [048](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-048).
- Sales growth base rates: median growth is modest. Analyst forecasts are too narrow. Very high sustained growth is historically unprecedented for large firms [083](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-083) [072](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-072) [075](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-075) [070](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-070) [145](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-145) [140](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-140). Apply this to aggressive AI-era forecasts and update as evidence arrives [005](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-005) [003](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-003).
- Update priors with Bayes' rule. People routinely neglect base rates [103](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-103) [003](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-003).
- Drawdowns and large price moves have their own base rates. Deep declines are common even in strong businesses [009](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-009) [086](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-086) [073](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-073).

## 11. Scenario probabilities and payoffs

- Results depend on both how often you are right and how much you make when you are, so quantify probabilities and payoffs explicitly [010](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-010) [148](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-148) [137](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-137).
- Use expected value with a margin of safety. Size positions by edge (Kelly-style), with fractional sizing when inputs are uncertain [124](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-124) [149](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-149) [010](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-010).
- Volatility drag and fat tails make compounding differ from arithmetic averages. A few rare outcomes dominate results [010](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-010) [142](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-142) [146](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-146) [169](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-169).

## 12. Decision quality

- Judge decisions by process, because outcomes mix skill and luck [109](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-109) [098](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-098) [137](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-137) [102](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-102).
- Forecast in explicit probabilities, score yourself with Brier scores, keep a decision journal, and run premortems [077](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-077) [094](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-094) [034](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-034) [082](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-082) [048](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-048).
- Calibrate confidence separately from probability [024](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-024) [082](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-082).
- Ask who is on the other side of a trade. Edges are behavioural, analytical, informational or technical [006](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-006) [050](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-050) [054](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-054) [084](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-084).
- Markets are complex adaptive systems: crowds are wise when diversity holds, and fragile when it breaks down [001](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-001) [085](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-085) [115](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-115) [119](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-119) [178](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-178) [158](notes/notes-123-183.md#paper-158).
- Teams work best small, diverse and psychologically safe, with independent voting [096](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-096) [055](notes/notes-001-061.md#paper-055) [067](notes/notes-062-122.md#paper-067).
