## 001 · The Wisdom of Crowds in Markets: Crowd Behavior in Prediction, Betting, and Stock Markets

**Listed:** 2026 · Counterpoint Global · Michael J. Mauboussin with Dan Callahan · printed 2026-08-05  
**Source:** [www.morganstanley.com](https://www.morganstanley.com/content/dam/im/assets/publication/thought-leadership/consilient-observer/article_thewisdomofcrowds_ltr.pdf)

**Question.** When do crowds price outcomes well across prediction, sports-betting, parimutuel and stock markets, and what breaks that accuracy?

**Argument and evidence.**
- Election markets edged out the final poll in accuracy a week before elections (p. 10).
- Gains in prediction markets are highly concentrated among a small minority of profitable users while most participants lose (p. 13).
- Transaction costs mean only a small share of sports bettors profit over time (p. 18).
- Lab markets with long horizons converge on fundamental value, while short-horizon markets are prone to bubbles (p. 28).

**Application.** Treat crowd prices as probability signals but check the three conditions (diversity, aggregation, incentives); extreme sentiment and valuation are symptoms of lost diversity. A long horizon helps equity investors because stocks carry a positive expected return, unlike zero-sum betting.

**Limitations.** U.S.-centred, several figures rest on single studies, and the regulatory backdrop for prediction markets is changing.

*Access:* web fetch of publisher PDF; first 100k of 186k characters read. Reviewed 2026-11-23.

## 002 · Opportunities and Expectations: The Present Value of Growth Opportunities in Valuation

**Listed:** 2026 · Counterpoint Global · Michael J. Mauboussin with Dan Callahan · printed 2026-06-18  
**Source:** [www.morganstanley.com](https://www.morganstanley.com/content/dam/im/assets/publication/thought-leadership/consilient-observer/article_opportunitiesandexpectations_ltr.pdf)

**Question.** Does the share of price attributable to the present value of growth opportunities (PVGO) measure expectations usefully for markets and individual stocks?

**Argument and evidence.**
- Market-level PVGO share averaged roughly a third of price over 1961-2025 and stood well above that at end-2025 (p. 3).
- Its link to subsequent ten-year returns is modest and informative mainly at extremes (p. 4).
- Low-PVGO companies delivered higher median five-year TSR than high-PVGO companies (p. 5), and a low-minus-high spread beat a conventional value factor (p. 8).

**Application.** Split price into steady-state value and PVGO to see how much future value creation is already paid for; use extreme readings as a prompt for scrutiny, not a timing rule.

**Limitations.** Sensitive to the cost of capital and earnings inputs; one prior study could not be replicated (p. 14 notes).

*Access:* web fetch of publisher PDF; full text. Reviewed 2026-11-23.

## 003 · Bayes and Base Rates 2.0: How History Can Guide Our Assessment of the Future

**Listed:** 2026 · Counterpoint Global · Michael J. Mauboussin with Dan Callahan · printed 2026-05-13  
**Source:** [www.morganstanley.com](https://www.morganstanley.com/content/dam/im/assets/publication/thought-leadership/consilient-observer/article_bayesandbaserates2_ltr.pdf)

**Question.** Are very high forecast sales growth rates for a leading AI developer plausible against historical base rates, and do the base rates still apply to a new general-purpose technology?

**Argument and evidence.**
- No U.S. public company since 1950 has sustained comparable five-year growth; the closest case was merger-assisted (p. 1).
- Against a mean five-year CAGR near 7% and a standard deviation near 11%, the forecast sits many standard deviations out (p. 3).
- Restricting to sectors does not lift the tail, and most top growth cases involved M&A (pp. 5-7).

**Application.** Use the base rate as a prior and update with evidence; separate organic from acquired growth and weigh growth against the returns on the investment needed to achieve it.

**Limitations.** Relies on press-reported forecasts; the normal approximation is illustrative; reference-class choice is debatable for a new technology (p. 5).

**Editions.** Revision of 005: adds real-terms results, sector cuts and updated forecasts (pp. 1, 3, 6).

*Access:* web fetch of publisher PDF; full text. Reviewed 2026-11-23.

## 004 · Competitive Advantage Period: The Neglected Value Driver

**Listed:** 2026 · Counterpoint Global · Michael J. Mauboussin with Dan Callahan · printed 2026-04-14  
**Source:** [www.morganstanley.com](https://www.morganstanley.com/content/dam/im/assets/publication/thought-leadership/consilient-observer/article_theneglectedvaluedriver_ltr.pdf)

**Question.** How long can a company earn returns above its cost of capital (competitive advantage period), and what period does the current price imply?

**Argument and evidence.**
- Most U.S. nonfinancial companies sit in growth or maturity stages where a CAP-based DCF fits best (p. 26).
- Sector ROIC correlations imply fade rates around 0.2 per year with a 0.1-0.3 range (p. 21).
- Sales growth persists weakly; few very fast growers sustain their pace and many turn negative (pp. 30-31).

**Application.** Begin with consensus, test growth against base rates and incremental ROIC, then extend the explicit forecast until value matches price; the implied CAP is the expectation to judge.

**Limitations.** U.S. data and sector averages; correlation-based fade estimates are noisy (p. 21). Terminal-value section and case study beyond the first 100k characters were not read in this pass.

*Access:* web fetch of publisher PDF; first 100k of 220k characters read (about pp. 1-32). Reviewed 2026-11-23.

## 005 · Bayes and Base Rates: How History Can Guide Our Assessment of the Future

**Listed:** 2026 · Counterpoint Global · Michael J. Mauboussin with Dan Callahan · printed 2026-02-10  
**Source:** [www.morganstanley.com](https://www.morganstanley.com/content/dam/im/assets/publication/thought-leadership/consilient-observer/article_bayesandbaserates_ltr.pdf)

**Question.** How plausible are the aggressive growth forecasts of a leading AI developer and a large cloud business against base rates?

**Argument and evidence.**
- The forecast implies more than 100% compound annual growth over five years (p. 3).
- No U.S. public company of comparable starting size grew that fast since 1950; the average five-year growth was about 7% (pp. 4-5).
- Very large projects rarely finish on time and budget, and very few also deliver expected benefits (pp. 7-8).

**Application.** Start with a low prior for extreme growth claims and update as evidence arrives; watch power, chips, financing and build schedules as the gating constraints.

**Limitations.** Public-company base rates may not fit private firms; third-party data unverified.

**Editions.** Earlier edition of 003 (nominal data only).

## 006 · Who Is On the Other Side? A Framework for Understanding Market (In)Efficiency

**Source:** [www.morganstanley.com](https://www.morganstanley.com/content/dam/im/assets/publication/thought-leadership/consilient-observer/article_whoisontheotherside.pdf)

**Question.** What determines market efficiency, and where do exploitable mispricings come from?

**Argument and evidence.**
- Long-run U.S. stock returns are highly skewed: most stocks underperform bills and a small fraction creates most wealth (p. 7).

- Mutual-fund gross alpha roughly equals fees (p. 11); individuals underperform while institutions show more analytical skill (p. 33).

**Limitations.** U.S.-focused; conclusion, checklist and references beyond the first 100k characters were not read in this pass.

## 007 · Capital Allocation: Results, Analysis, and Assessment (Updated)

**Listed:** 2025 · Counterpoint Global · Michael J. Mauboussin with Dan Callahan · printed 2025-11-05  
**Source:** [www.morganstanley.com](https://www.morganstanley.com/content/dam/im/assets/publication/thought-leadership/consilient-observer/article_capitalallocation.pdf)

**Question.** How well do U.S. companies allocate capital, and what distinguishes good allocation?

**Argument and evidence.**
- Firms reallocate capital across units too little; almost none exceed the optimal level (p. 4).
- Buybacks overtook dividends while dividends stayed far less volatile (pp. 11, 35).
- Cash deals outperform stock deals, and acquirer shares fall on announcement more often than not (pp. 17, 20); divestitures and spinoffs tend to add value (p. 34).

**Application.** Size deal risk as premium relative to acquirer market cap, weigh payment method and synergy credibility, and question large excess cash balances.

**Limitations.** Averages hide dispersion; intangible estimates depend on assumptions (p. 24); text beyond about p. 40 not read in this pass.

- Aggregate cash-to-assets is above its 1970-2024 average (p. 3).

## 009 · Drawdowns and Recoveries: Base Rates for Bottoms and Bounces

**Listed:** 2025 · Counterpoint Global · Michael J. Mauboussin with Dan Callahan · printed 2025-05-21  
**Source:** [www.morganstanley.com](https://www.morganstanley.com/content/dam/im/assets/publication/thought-leadership/consilient-observer/article_drawdownsandrecoveries_ltr.pdf)

**Question.** How deep and long are individual-stock drawdowns, and what follows them?

**Argument and evidence.**
- The median U.S. stock's maximum drawdown is severe and over half never regain the prior peak (p. 4).
- Deeper drawdowns recover less often, though rebounds from the deepest bins are larger when they happen (p. 6).
- Even a hindsight portfolio of the best performers suffered a deep drawdown (p. 10).

**Application.** Expect large declines in good holdings; before buying a fallen stock, distinguish cyclical from secular causes and check balance-sheet strength and capital access (pp. 17-19).

**Limitations.** Hindsight sample excludes delisted firms (p. 3); troughs are not identifiable in real time.

## 010 · Probabilities and Payoffs: The Practicalities and Psychology of Expected Value

**Listed:** 2025 · Counterpoint Global · Michael J. Mauboussin with Dan Callahan · printed 2025-02-19  
**Source:** [www.morganstanley.com](https://www.morganstanley.com/content/dam/im/assets/publication/thought-leadership/consilient-observer/article_probabilitiesandpayoffs.pdf)

**Question.** How should investors think about probabilities, payoffs and expected value to earn excess returns?

**Argument and evidence.**
- Excess returns depend on both hit rate and payoff size; venture investing loses on most deals yet profits overall (pp. 1, 28).
- Kelly sizing ties bet size to edge and overbetting reduces growth (p. 19).
- Positive expected value does not guarantee compounding; volatility drag separates arithmetic and geometric returns (pp. 16, 21).

**Application.** Quantify scenarios explicitly, size positions with edge and uncertainty in mind, score forecasts, start from base rates and keep a margin of safety.

**Limitations.** Kelly needs reliable inputs and is volatile (p. 20); some samples have survivorship features (p. 21).

*Access:* web fetch of publisher PDF; first 100k of 162k characters read. Reviewed 2026-11-23.

**Source:** [www.morganstanley.com](https://www.morganstanley.com/im/publication/insights/articles/article_measuringthemoat.pdf)

- ROICs revert substantially toward the mean (p. 3).

## 015 · Valuation Multiples: What They Miss, Why They Differ, and the Link to Fundamentals

**Listed:** 2024 · Counterpoint Global · Michael J. Mauboussin with Dan Callahan · printed 2024-04-23  
**Source:** [12mv2.com](https://12mv2.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/24-04-23-valuation-multiples__what-they-miss-why-they-differ-and-the-link-to-fundamentals.pdf)

**Question.** What do valuation multiples miss, why do P/E and EV/EBITDA diverge, and how do they link to fundamentals?

**Argument and evidence.**
- Multiples compress a DCF and hide whether investment earns above the cost of capital (p. 3).
- Expensed intangibles distort earnings; an illustrative adjustment lifts a large software company's EBITDA substantially and lowers its multiple (p. 4).
- Leverage, taxes and depreciation explain P/E versus EV/EBITDA gaps (pp. 6-9).

**Application.** Use multiples as shorthand only after checking ROIC, growth, leverage, tax and D&A intensity; compare with warranted multiples (p. 15) and treat stock compensation as a real expense (p. 10).

**Limitations.** Simplified illustrations; intangible adjustments need judgment (p. 4 n. 8).

**Source:** [12mv2.com](https://12mv2.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/23-03-22-confidence__methods-to-assess-confidence-under-uncertainty.pdf)

**Question.** How can investors assess confidence in a probability separately from the probability itself?

**Argument and evidence.**
- Probability and confidence are distinct concepts (p. 1).
- People overweight the strength of evidence relative to its weight, producing overconfidence on small samples (p. 4).
- More information raised forecasters' confidence without improving accuracy (p. 6).

**Application.** Rate evidence reliability, the range of reasonable opinion and responsiveness to new information, and use the result in ranking, sizing and diversification.

**Limitations.** Qualitative framework with no numeric confidence measure.

## 025 · Cost of Capital: A Practical Guide to Measuring Opportunity Cost

**Listed:** 2023 · Counterpoint Global · Michael J. Mauboussin with Dan Callahan · printed 2023-02-15  
**Source:** [12mv2.com](https://12mv2.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/23-02-15-cost-of-capital__a-practical-guide-to-measuring-opportunity-cost.pdf)

**Question.** How should an investor estimate a company's weighted average cost of capital?

**Argument and evidence.**

**Listed:** 2017 · Blue Mountain · Michael J. Mauboussin · printed 2017-12-20  
**Source:** [12mv2.com](https://12mv2.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/17-12-20-how-well-do-you-compare.pdf)

**Question.** How can investors compare companies more effectively and select better peers?

**Argument and evidence.**
- Memory-based analogies overweight salient similarities (pp. 3-4).
- Analysts tend to pick high-valuation peers to make a stock look cheap (p. 8).
- Value-driver similarity scores find peers outside standard industry codes (p. 12).

**Application.** Select peers by economic similarity in value drivers and use similarity-based forecasting (pp. 9-12).

**Limitations.** Little performance evidence (pp. 12, 16).

## 054 · Winning the Easy Game: Skill and the Ability to Extract Value

**Listed:** 2017 · Credit Suisse · Michael J. Mauboussin · printed 2017-06-11  
**Source:** [12mv2.com](https://12mv2.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/17-06-11-winning-the-easy-game__skill-and-the-ability-to-extract-value.pdf)

**Question.** Where should the balance between active and passive investing settle, given costs and benefits?

**Argument and evidence.**
- Active managers' gross profit roughly matches fees, consistent with efficiently inefficient markets (p. 7).
- Gross profit persists while net alpha is near zero (p. 13).
- Asset-weighted returns beat equal-weighted returns (p. 11).

**Application.** Use active management where skill can be assessed and gross profit varies (p. 15).

**Limitations.** Ignores flows; excludes ETFs (pp. 5, 13).

## 055 · Assembling a Dream Team: How Gender Diversity Can Strengthen Your Team

**Listed:** 2017 · Credit Suisse · Michael J. Mauboussin · printed 2017-05-11  
**Source:** [12mv2.com](https://12mv2.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/17-05-11-assembling-a-dream-team__how-gender-diversity-can-strengthen-your-team.pdf)

**Question.** Do team-managed funds outperform and what makes investment teams work?

**Argument and evidence.**
- Small teams, around three, showed the best risk-adjusted gains (p. 2).
- Cognitive diversity helps, with team composition effects observed (p. 6).
- Psychological safety and dependability predict team success (p. 6).

**Application.** Favour small, odd-numbered teams, structured interviews and norms that make dissent safe (pp. 5, 14).

**Limitations.** Diversity measured by proxies; small samples for large teams (pp. 5-6).

**Question.** Why has the U.S. listed-company count fallen since 1996 and what does it mean for investors?

**Argument and evidence.**
- U.S. listings fell about half while other developed markets rose (p. 4).
- M&A is the main reason for delisting and IPOs no longer replace them (pp. 6, 11).
- Remaining listed firms are older and more profitable (p. 9).

**Application.** Investors may need private-market access to reach the full opportunity set (pp. 3, 18-19).

**Limitations.** Gap estimates rely on models (pp. 4-5).

**Question.** How can investors judge whether an announced acquisition will create value?

**Argument and evidence.**
- M&A creates value in aggregate but sellers capture most of it (p. 2).
- EPS accretion is a weak guide to value creation (p. 12).
- Cash deals fare better than stock deals around announcement (p. 13).

**Application.** Assess materiality (shareholder value at risk), synergy value versus premium, the financing signal and the strategic category (p. 7).

**Limitations.** Small recent sample; management synergy estimates taken as given (p. 12).

## 058 · Corporate Longevity: Index Turnover and Corporate Performance

**Listed:** 2017 · Credit Suisse · Michael J. Mauboussin · printed 2017-02-07  
**Source:** [12mv2.com](https://12mv2.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/17-02-07-corporate-longevity__index-turnover-and-corporate-performance.pdf)

**Question.** What drives S&P 500 turnover and does it reflect creative destruction?

**Argument and evidence.**
- Index turnover tracks M&A activity more closely than an innovation measure (pp. 6-7).
- Removed stocks that keep trading outperform additions over the following year (p. 9).
- Failure-driven removals show weak economic returns afterward (p. 10).

**Application.** Account for benchmark changes and beware value traps among cheap, failed companies.

**Limitations.** Correlation not causation; committee discretion.

**Editions.** Successor to 093 (2014).

**Question.** Does time (Moore's law) or cumulative output (Wright's law) better explain technology cost declines?

**Argument and evidence.**
- Across many technologies, Wright's law forecasts best with Moore's law close behind (p. 8).
- The two fit similarly when output grows exponentially (p. 5).
- Battery costs fell steeply over 2008-2015 (p. 9).

**Application.** Track cumulative output and capacity as leading indicators of future unit costs (p. 9).

## 060 · Looking for Easy Games: How Passive Investing Shapes Active Management

**Listed:** 2017 · Credit Suisse · Michael J. Mauboussin · printed 2017-01-04  
**Source:** [12mv2.com](https://12mv2.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/17-01-04-looking-for-easy-games__how-passive-investing-shapes-active-management.pdf)

**Question.** Why are investors moving from active to passive management and what does that mean for active managers?

**Argument and evidence.**
- Large outflows from active equity funds were matched by inflows to index funds and ETFs (p. 4).
- Few active funds beat a broad benchmark over five years (p. 6).
- Average fund fees fell markedly since 1990 (p. 17).

**Application.** Most individuals are well served by low-cost passive portfolios; sophisticated allocators should seek skilled managers in high-dispersion areas (p. 34).

**Limitations.** Historical data and illustrative models; later sections not read.

## 061 · Capital Allocation Outside the US: Evidence, Analytical Methods & Assessment Guidance

**Listed:** 2016 · Credit Suisse · Michael J. Mauboussin · printed 2016-11-15  
**Source:** [12mv2.com](https://12mv2.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/16-11-15-capital-allocation-outside-the-us__evidence-analytical-methods-assessment-guidance.pdf)

**Question.** How does capital allocation differ outside the U.S. and how can management skill be assessed?

**Argument and evidence.**
- Internal financing tracks ROIC across countries (p. 9).
- M&A intensity is far lower in Japan than in the U.S. and Europe (p. 23).
- Developing economies show no significant asset-growth effect (p. 10).

**Application.** Compare a company's spending mix with regional norms, test ROIC against growth and check incentives.

**Limitations.** Large firms only; data through 2015; Part III not read.

**Editions.** Companion to 087 (2015 edition) and the U.S. reports 63/81/90.
