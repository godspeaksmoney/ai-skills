# Finding the argument in the book

This guide follows the Stripe Press digital edition of *Poor Charlie’s Almanack*, edited by Peter D. Kaufman. Print locators refer to the 2023 fourth abridged edition (hardcover ISBN 978-1-953953-23-0). Follow a chapter link for the text, illustrations and notes. The publisher also offers a [continuous text view](https://stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/book). The questions below are applications developed for this skill.

Chapter 4 opens on print p. 69 and collects the eleven talks. The two forewords begin on pp. 13 and 17, Munger’s rebuttal on p. 21 and Kaufman’s introduction on p. 23. Chapters 1–3 begin on pp. 27, 41 and 53. ## Before the talks

| Section and voice | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| John B. Collison, Warren E. Buffett, Charles T. Munger, and Peter D. Kaufman | Collison describes the breadth of a conversation with Munger. Buffett discusses choosing a partner who can tolerate mistakes. Munger questions how much credit he deserves for Buffett’s evolution. Kaufman explains how stories and repetition build usable judgment. Distinguish these voices when attributing a claim. [Forewords, rebuttal and introduction](https://stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/forewords) |
| Chapter 1 · Michael Broggie’s portrait | The losses in the investment partnership make the owner’s funding arrangement a real part of the decision. Who must provide cash before an investment has time to work? [A Portrait of Charles T. Munger](https://stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/chapter-one) |
| Chapter 2 · Family recollections | The borrowed jeep returned with a full tank is a small, observable act of trustworthiness. Ask what people do when the beneficiary may never see the effort. Treat a family memory as one person’s evidence. [Remembering](https://stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/chapter-two) |
| Chapter 3 · Editorial account of Munger’s approach | Separate businesses the analyst understands from those beyond their competence. Then investigate whole-business economics, downside and alternatives. A checklist can catch omissions even when the output is a decision to pass. [The Munger Approach](https://stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/chapter-three) |

## The eleven talks

| Talk | Print start / revisit | A question the source helps answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Harvard School commencement | 73 / 80 | What habits would reliably produce the outcome you most want to avoid? Invert the problem, choose a preventable cause, and substitute a behavior that another person could observe. The revisit singles out reliability. [Talk One](https://stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/talk-one) |
| 2 · Elementary worldly wisdom | 83 / 116 (Q&A p. 119) | Who will capture the value of a better machine once rivals install it too? Compare the economics of a commodity producer and a local publisher. Use the betting discussion to separate the likely winner from the price on offer. The revisit and Q&A broaden the question to institutional investing, acquisitions and careers. [Talk Two](https://stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/talk-two) |
| 3 · Worldly wisdom revisited | 121 / 160 | Would you approve the original USAir preferred investment using only information available at the time? Eventual repayment can hide a weak decision. Compare conflicting expert opinions by seeking an independent explanation for both. The revisit extends the discussion to ideology and cheating. [Talk Three](https://stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/talk-three) |
| 4 · Practical thought | 163 / 177 | Can the target outcome be built from units, customer behavior, distribution and price? In the imagined beverage company, availability makes habit more valuable and habit makes distribution more productive. Test where the cycle could break. The revisit points back to the psychology in Talk 11. [Talk Four](https://stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/talk-four) |
| 5 · Harvard Law School reunion | 181 / 193 | Which alternative has disappeared because every specialist has framed the problem the same way? In the shoe-factory case a sale to a capable operator belongs beside a turnaround. The pilot-training analogy asks which rare, costly failures deserve rehearsal. [Talk Five](https://stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/talk-five) |
| 6 · Charitable foundations | 195 / 204 | How much can the beneficiary still spend in real terms once fees, turnover and distributions are paid? Compare the required active advantage with evidence that the institution can actually sustain it. Look through different managers for the same exposure. [Talk Six](https://stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/talk-six) |
| 7 · Philanthropy Roundtable | 207 / 216 | Who spends cash now, and whose ability to spend depends on a rising quoted value? Munger extends Galbraith’s “bezzle” idea to largely invisible investment friction. Trace the feedback if prices stall. His conjecture supplies a stress scenario rather than a forecast date. [Talk Seven](https://stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/talk-seven) |
| 8 · The Great Financial Scandal of 2003 | 219 / 231 | This is a fictional accounting parable written in 2000. Reconcile the economics of employee compensation with the published profit, the share count, and cash used for buybacks. Who benefits if reviewers accept the headline figure? [Talk Eight](https://stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/talk-eight) |
| 9 · Academic economics | 233 / 264 | When a price rise coincides with greater demand, test the end buyer’s inference of quality against the intermediary’s incentive to recommend the product. Identify who selects, who pays, and what each explanation predicts. [Talk Nine](https://stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/talk-nine) |
| 10 · USC law commencement | 267 | Can an apparent expert explain a changed premise? Use the Planck and chauffeur story, which Munger presents as apocryphal, to design a question that requires understanding. Consider where the work environment rewards candor or discourages it. [Talk Ten](https://stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/talk-ten) |
| 11 · Human misjudgment | 283 / 350 | Which pressures reinforce one another, and which decision rule would break their interaction? Use the concluding questions for examples of institutional design and the revisit for a case of revising received beliefs. Munger acknowledges his framework’s limitations. [Talk Eleven](https://stripe.press/poor-charlies-almanack/talk-eleven) |
