# Howard Marks memo reading notes, 2007–2017

Dates and titles follow Oaktree’s annual index. Page numbers refer to the individual linked PDFs. The applications are the analyst’s interpretation. Contemporaneous judgments and examples retain their historical dates.

## 2007

### 2007-02-14 · The Race to the Bottom

In an auction to lend, one lender can win by accepting a smaller spread, more leverage or weaker creditor rights. Interest that can be paid with more debt delays visible defaults and can make bad underwriting look sound. Judge the protections surrendered to win the deal alongside the coupon. (pp. 1–8) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2007-02-14-the-race-to-the-bottom.pdf?sfvrsn=9bbc0f65_6)

### 2007-04-26 · Everyone Knows

A widely admired business can be an expensive security because buyers have already paid for its virtues. Less celebrated, lower-rated debt once offered yields sufficient to absorb actual defaults. Compare the favorable outcome already in the quote with realistic cash receipts, and allow time for a disagreement with the crowd to resolve. (pp. 2–6, 8–10) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2007-04-26-everyone-knows.pdf?sfvrsn=d3bc0f65_6)

### 2007-07-16 · It’s All Good

Gains across unrelated asset classes pointed to a shared driver in easy financing and willingness to bear risk. Leverage made modest underlying returns look attractive; structuring could disguise sensitivity to the same collateral. Examine the investment unlevered and under the loss of cheap credit before asserting diversification. (pp. 3–11) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2007-07-16-its-all-good.pdf?sfvrsn=8fbc0f65_6)

### 2007-07-30 · It’s All Good . . . Really?

A problem initially described as confined to subprime mortgages spread via collateral values, structured-debt demand and margin calls. Forced sellers liquidated what still had buyers, allowing sound assets to fall alongside impaired ones. Trace the next compelled transaction and compare the resulting price with independently estimated value. (pp. 2–8) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2007-07-30-its-all-good-really.pdf?sfvrsn=97bc0f65_6)

### 2007-09-10 · Now It’s All Bad?

When demand from structured buyers diminished, banks could no longer distribute loans committed to buyouts. Credit withdrawn from one market choked off unrelated deals and reversed the feedback that had lifted prices. Identify the ultimate source of financing and the effect of its withdrawal, even for businesses with little direct mortgage exposure. (pp. 2–10, 12–14) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2007-09-10-now-its-all-bad.pdf?sfvrsn=9fbc0f65_6)

### 2007-12-17 · No Different This Time – The Lessons of ‘07

A low-return opportunity set encouraged investors to borrow against a thin spread until it met their target. The exercise enlarged downside exposure without improving the asset. Compare unlevered economics, funding terms and joint tail losses. Allow the option of a lower target return when sound opportunities offer less. (pp. 2–6, 8–12) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2007-12-17-no-different-this-time-the-lessons-of-07.pdf?sfvrsn=f3bc0f65_6)

## 2008

### 2008-01-10 · Now What?

The flow of new CLO money had supported loan prices. When that buyer disappeared, banks were left with inventory and some funds suspended redemptions. Weaker covenants could postpone an LBO default without improving repayment prospects. Map financing maturities and the likely marginal buyer before calling a decline a bargain. (pp. 3–5, 10–14) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2008-01-10-now-what.pdf?sfvrsn=d7bc0f65_6)

### 2008-02-20 · Whodunit

Mortgage brokers could earn a fee at origination and pass the credit exposure to buyers who relied on ratings. Slicing these loans into tranches redistributed losses while leaving the collateral pool unchanged. Follow a transaction from origination to the ultimate loss bearer and investigate whose compensation depended on loan quality. (pp. 1–7, 10–12) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2008-02-20-whodunit.pdf?sfvrsn=e7bc0f65_6)

### 2008-03-18 · The Tide Goes Out

A falling mark reduced a leveraged holder’s equity, prompting margin calls and sales that reset prices for other holders. What worked through a full cycle could still ruin an investor during it. Stress the collateral and leverage loop, then ask whether current sellers are expressing a view or meeting a demand for cash. (pp. 1–8, 11–13) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2008-03-18-the-tide-goes-out.pdf?sfvrsn=e3bc0f65_6)

### 2008-05-16 · The Aviary

The crisis undermined faith in market self-policing, yet rules can lag the next innovation and stress tests can reproduce yesterday’s blind spots. Mortgage losses also damaged bank capital and credit available to unrelated businesses. Model indirect transmission and retain a cushion for outcomes absent from the historical sample. (pp. 2–11, 13–15) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2008-05-16-the-aviary.pdf?sfvrsn=efbc0f65_6)

### 2008-07-31 · Doesn’t Make Sense

A firm could book an accounting gain when the market price of its own debt fell because investors expected worse repayment. Headline earnings and issuer-paid ratings could therefore give perverse signals. Reconcile reported profit to cash obligations and ask who pays the intermediary making the risk assessment. (pp. 3–7, 10–12) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2008-07-31-doesnt-make-sense.pdf?sfvrsn=ebbc0f65_6)

### 2008-08-28 · What Worries Me

Marks questions how long U.S. spending could outpace income as aging, pension and health obligations grew and global competition pressured wages. Borrowing can bridge the gap for years and create an illusion of sustainable demand. For a consumer business, stress demand against cash earnings and savings rather than credit-fueled sales alone. (pp. 3–9, 12–17) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2008-08-28-what-worries-me.pdf?sfvrsn=f7bc0f65_6)

### 2008-09-19 · Nobody Knows

In the week of Lehman’s failure, uncertainty about counterparties threatened the functioning of credit itself. A blanket assumption that the financial system would fail was as consequential as earlier faith in safe leverage. Examine essential businesses security by security for prices that compensate even under extended stress. (pp. 2–7) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2008-09-19-nobody-knows.pdf?sfvrsn=cbbc0f65_6)

### 2008-09-24 · Plan B

The proposed U.S. rescue faced a pricing dilemma. Paying too little for troubled assets would leave banks impaired, while paying too much transferred value to private holders. Counterparty chains made waiting costly. For any rescue, specify who provides capital, who receives it, the purchase terms and which claim has priority. (pp. 1–8) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2008-09-24-plan-b.pdf?sfvrsn=ffbc0f65_6)

### 2008-10-15 · The Limits to Negativism

By October, ever darker scenarios were accepted more readily than plausible recoveries. An investment could offer a large margin of safety before the economy turned. Anchor purchases to a reasoned estimate of recoverable value and retain the capital to add at lower prices if fresh evidence supports that estimate. (pp. 1–5, 8–9) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2008-10-15-the-limits-to-negativism.pdf?sfvrsn=fbbc0f65_6)

### 2008-12-17 · Volatility + Leverage = Dynamite

Permanent financing magnifies returns, while callable funding can impose liquidation at a temporary low. Even an economically hedged institution can be exposed to its hedge counterparty failing. Estimate the asset decline that exhausts equity and the smaller move that triggers a collateral call or funding withdrawal. (pp. 2–8, 10–12) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2008-12-17-volatility-leverage-dynamite.pdf?sfvrsn=c7bc0f65_6)

## 2009

### 2009-01-09 · The Long View

A career spanning only part of a multidecade expansion can feel like a full historical sample. The gradual normalization of revolving credit, low down payments and household leverage helped consumption for years and made reversal more damaging. Reconstruct the long trend behind a through-cycle earnings estimate. (pp. 2–9, 12–17) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2009-01-09-the-long-view.pdf?sfvrsn=c3bc0f65_6)

### 2009-03-05 · Will It Work?

Households and governments cutting spending can each improve their own finances while deepening an aggregate contraction. Crisis programs therefore entail competing aims, political constraints and uncertain responses. Underwrite the security’s survivability if the proposed rescue is delayed, partial or differently designed. (pp. 2–11) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2009-03-05-will-it-work.pdf?sfvrsn=dbbc0f65_8)

### 2009-07-08 · So Much That’s False and Nutty

The crisis exposed mismatches between liquidity promises and commitments, incentives and risk borne, and asset complexity and buyer diligence. An endowment could overcommit even without a conventional borrowing balance. Add contingent commitments to the liquidity schedule and judge fees against skill after accounting for leverage. (pp. 5–13) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2009-07-08-so-much-thats-false-nutty.pdf?sfvrsn=23bb0f65_6)

### 2009-11-10 · Touchstones

Housing appreciation enabled more borrowing and buying, temporarily validating the premise that made leverage seem safe. Once credit tightened, that feedback ran backward. Separate operating value from value that depends on rising collateral, and check that funding survives every point on the path to recovery. (pp. 2–9, 11–13) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2009-11-10-touchstones.pdf?sfvrsn=2fbb0f65_6)

## 2010

### 2010-01-22 · Tell Me I’m Wrong

After the 2009 rebound, economic recovery depended partly on cheap funding and government stimulus. Productivity improvements could raise company earnings while depressing employment and consumer demand. Recalculate normalized demand and funding costs before extrapolating crisis-rebound profits into a now-higher purchase price. (pp. 1–5, 8–12) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2010-01-22-tell-me-im-wrong.pdf?sfvrsn=2bbb0f65_6)

### 2010-03-17 · I’d Rather Be Wrong

The U.S. and California show how veto points and political incentives can prevent an arithmetically necessary fiscal adjustment. A proposed bipartisan commission offers limited protection if its recommendations cannot pass. In a credit case, identify who can authorize the tax increase or spending cut your repayment case requires. (pp. 1–5) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2010-03-17-id-rather-be-wrong.pdf?sfvrsn=fbb0f65_6)

### 2010-05-12 · Warning Flags

Within a year of the credit rebound, dividend recapitalizations, payment-in-kind debt and loose covenants returned. A sponsor’s cash dividend could improve its own near-term return by weakening the borrower. Treat changes in creditor protections and the destination of new borrowing as observable warning signs before a catalyst appears. (pp. 4–12) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2010-05-12-warning-flags.pdf?sfvrsn=dfbc0f65_6)

### 2010-07-19 · It’s Greek to Me

Greece shared monetary policy with stronger European neighbors while bearing domestic fiscal obligations. Rescue loans could buy time, but austerity in a weak economy could make the debt burden harder to shrink. Separate currency control, solvency and refinancing, and stress the impact of the remedy on the borrower’s income. (pp. 2–10, 13–17) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2010-07-19-its-greek-to-me.pdf?sfvrsn=27bb0f65_6)

### 2010-09-10 · Hemlines

Investors had swung from favoring equities after long gains to bidding up bonds following strong fixed-income returns. The second group locked in a low yield just as it felt safest. Compare the forward yield and valuation of both assets, and identify what their divergent histories already did to the entry price. (pp. 2–10) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2010-09-10-hemlines.pdf?sfvrsn=7bb0f65_6)

### 2010-12-01 · Open and Shut

Credit supply can swing more than the economy. Easy finance rescues weak borrowers and raises purchase prices, then lenders retreat together and force otherwise viable borrowers into distress. Separate operating viability from dependence on a permanently open refinancing window. Capital and patience matter most when that window closes. (pp. 1–8) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2010-12-01-open-and-shut.pdf?sfvrsn=67bb0f65_6)

### 2010-12-17 · All That Glitters

Gold’s history as a store of value can justify some demand for protection, but it has no stream of income to discount. Even a persuasive argument for ownership gives little guidance on the price to pay. Specify the portfolio purpose and valuation uncertainty before treating appreciation as proof of an attractive entry. (pp. 2–5, 7–11) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2010-12-17-all-that-glitters.pdf?sfvrsn=3bb0f65_6)

## 2011

### 2011-03-02 · On Regulation

Regulatory cycles respond to perceived excess and then to the constraints regulation creates. The rating agencies’ incentives and the ability to hedge stock grants show why formally compliant behavior can defeat the economic purpose of a rule. Trace who benefits, who can adapt and how the regulator might respond. (pp. 2–8) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2011-03-02-on-regulation.pdf?sfvrsn=37bb0f65_6)

### 2011-05-25 · How Quickly They Forget

Within a few years of the crisis, debt-funded buyouts again needed less sponsor equity. Low safe yields made risk taking attractive even to wary lenders. Compare the current equity cushion and protections with earlier vintages, and consider that a normal spread over a tiny base can still give an inadequate absolute return. (pp. 5–12) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2011-05-25-how-quickly-they-forget.pdf?sfvrsn=33bb0f65_6)

### 2011-07-21 · Down to the Wire

A debt ceiling causes a visible deadline. The underlying imbalance arises when obligations grow faster than the resources available to service them, while credit masks the gap. For a sovereign-dependent issuer, distinguish a refinancing extension from a durable change to spending, revenues or growth. (pp. 2–7, 10–12) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2011-07-21-down-to-the-wire.pdf?sfvrsn=3fbb0f65_6)

### 2011-09-07 · What's Behind the Downturn?

The U.S. downgrade, European debt strains and weakening growth arrived together, changing how investors interpreted risks they had tolerated separately. Cheaper prices added protection even as prospective economic growth weakened. Re-underwrite cash flows and the purchase multiple independently before changing the position. (pp. 2–12) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2011-09-07-whats-behind-the-downturn.pdf?sfvrsn=13bb0f65_6)

### 2011-11-16 · It's All Very Taxing

A nominal tax rate leaves out deductions, different treatment of labor and gains, and behavioral changes in timing or reporting. Judgments about a fair distribution involve value choices as well as arithmetic. Model effective cash taxes and incentives under alternative policy packages, with the political choice explicit. (pp. 3–10, 13–18) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2011-11-16-its-all-very-taxing.pdf?sfvrsn=3bbb0f65_6)

## 2012

### 2012-01-10 · What Can We Do For You?

An active manager can estimate value, reject an overpriced offering, recognize poor terms and adjust exposure without spotting the next market turn. In 2012 Marks weighs a difficult macro picture against wide spreads and subdued sentiment. Evaluate a manager by the decisions available at the time and the price paid for uncertainty. (pp. 2–10) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2012-01-10-what-can-we-do-for-you.pdf?sfvrsn=7fbb0f65_8)

### 2012-02-15 · Assessing Performance Records A Case Study

Penn’s defensive endowment policy looked deficient during a boom and valuable through the financial crisis. Moving either endpoint changes the apparent verdict even over a decade. Evaluate drawdowns against the institution’s cash needs and compare the same strategy over full cycles before crediting skill. (pp. 1–7) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2012-02-15-assessing-performance-records-a-case-study.pdf?sfvrsn=6bbb0f65_6)

### 2012-03-19 · Déjà Vu All Over Again

The earlier “death of equities” narrative and later enthusiasm for permanent equity allocations both took cues from the returns that had just occurred. Cheap entry valuations can make a poor recent record the precondition for better returns. Ask whether the pricing today already capitalizes the recent winning trend. (pp. 2–10) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2012-03-19-d%C3%A9j%C3%A0-vu-all-over-again.pdf?sfvrsn=bbb0f65_6)

### 2012-06-20 · It's All A Big Mistake

For an active trade to have an edge, someone else generally has to be mispricing it. Mandate constraints, psychological pressures and imperfect hedges provide possible mechanisms. Identify the particular counterparty error and explain why your own model or hedge could be wrong, using the JPMorgan trading loss as a warning about certainty. (pp. 2–9) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2012-06-20-its-all-a-big-mistake.pdf?sfvrsn=17bb0f65_6)

### 2012-09-11 · On Uncertain Ground

Debt-driven demand, policy intervention and Europe’s unresolved problems made the outlook precarious, while fear had lowered some entry prices. A flight to perceived safety could lock in unattractive returns. Use macro fragility to favor dependable cash flows when prices allow, and compare the implied return on hiding in cash or safe bonds. (pp. 2–7, 9–14) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2012-09-11-on-uncertain-ground.pdf?sfvrsn=1fbb0f65_8)

### 2012-11-19 · A Fresh Start (Hopefully)

After the U.S. election, Marks contrasts divided voter preferences with the need for a workable fiscal compromise. The final presidential address is his imagined response. Treat a politician’s mandate, the votes needed to pass legislation and the economic effect of implementation as three distinct assumptions. (pp. 2–8) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2012-11-09-a-fresh-start-hopefully.pdf?sfvrsn=1bbb0f65_5)

## 2013

### 2013-01-07 · Ditto

Property construction lags demand, creating the capacity that can reverse a rent boom. Investment cycles have similar internal causes. Gains reduce perceived risk, fresh demand bids up prices, and the last buyer leaves fewer sources of upside. Check how today’s attractive returns change future supply, bidding pressure and expected return. (pp. 2–6, 9–12) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2013-01-07-ditto.pdf?sfvrsn=73bb0f65_6)

### 2013-02-21 · High Yield Bonds Today

Co-written with Sheldon Stone. At comparable maturities, larger coupons shorten duration. The authors compared the credit spread with potential default losses, including an illustrative 5.7% yield less 0.7% annual credit loss. For a bond, compare prospective net return and break-even defaults with the safer alternative. Re-estimate both inputs today. (pp. 1–2) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2013-02-21-high-yield-bonds-today.pdf?sfvrsn=63bb0f65_6)

### 2013-03-13 · The Outlook For Equities

An equity premium calculated from past returns tells little about the premium buyers will earn at today’s price. Marks weighs starting multiples, depressed public enthusiasm, historically high profit margins and the low Treasury yield. Model cash growth and valuation separately, and compare the security with alternatives actually available. (pp. 1–9) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2013-03-13-the-outlook-for-equities.pdf?sfvrsn=6fbb0f65_6)

### 2013-08-05 · The Role of Confidence

Confidence can create the conditions that appear to validate it. Rising paper wealth encourages spending and lending, which support growth. The same confidence can lead buyers to skip diligence and load up on leverage. Check how much of current demand or profitability would survive if credit confidence weakened. (pp. 1–7, 9–11) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2013-08-05-the-role-of-confidence.pdf?sfvrsn=7bbb0f65_6)

### 2013-11-26 · The Race Is On

Competition to deploy money had returned after the crisis. Investor behavior driven by a need for yield could produce loose terms even in the absence of widespread exuberance. Compare structures, issuance quality and sizing with previous vintages, and distinguish a worsening trend from evidence that the market has reached an extreme. (pp. 1–8) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2013-11-26-the-race-is-on.pdf?sfvrsn=77bb0f65_6)

## 2014

### 2014-01-16 · Getting Lucky

Marks traces his entry into convertibles and high-yield bonds to accidents of career timing that placed him in overlooked markets. He also shows why a spectacular single forecast proves little. Attribute results to the starting opportunity, repeatable decisions, luck and the time the investor could afford to wait. (pp. 3–11) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2014-01-16-getting-lucky.pdf?sfvrsn=c4b70f65_6)

### 2014-04-08 · Dare to Be Great II

Outperformance requires differing from the consensus. Acting differently also exposes a manager to looking foolish long before an investment resolves. The incentives facing investment committees and Oaktree’s early distressed funds frame a practical question for a contrarian. Can the mandate and its sponsors support the holding through interim underperformance? (pp. 2–9) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2014-04-08-dare-to-be-great-ii.pdf?sfvrsn=44b60f65_6)

### 2014-09-03 · Risk Revisited

Higher risk widens the range of outcomes, including the possibility of a serious loss. Only one outcome ultimately appears, concealing much of the original uncertainty. Map the paths to permanent loss, including collateral calls or covenant transfers, and identify how these exposures correlate when the portfolio is under stress. (pp. 3–12) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2014-09-03-risk-revisited.pdf?sfvrsn=78b60f65_6)

### 2014-12-18 · The Lessons of Oil

The oil collapse hurt producers and lenders, yet indiscriminate selling extended to countries that imported oil. Cheap fuel could help some issuers whose bonds fell alongside oil-dependent borrowers. Trace exposures through costs, customers and financing, then compare the price decline with the plausible cash loss for each security. (pp. 2–6) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2014-12-18-the-lessons-of-oil.pdf?sfvrsn=40b60f65_6)

## 2015

### 2015-03-25 · Liquidity

A quoted price and the ability to sell size near that price are different promises. Auction-rate securities stopped clearing when bidders disappeared, while AIG’s collateral calls made the mismatch between its liquid assets and its immediate obligations existential. Match dated cash needs and redemption terms to what the assets could actually fetch in a crowded sale. (pp. 2–5, 8–11) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2015-03-25-liquidity.pdf?sfvrsn=2dc70f65_6)

### 2015-06-08 · Risk Revisited Again

Two investments with the same expected payoff can have radically different left tails. Defaults that cluster in one year can destroy a structure designed around an accurate long-run average. Inventory funding, concentration, illiquidity, missed upside and behavioral risks together, then ask which risks the price pays the investor to bear. (pp. 5–12, 16–19) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2015-06-08-risk-revisited-again.pdf?sfvrsn=7bb70f65_8)

### 2015-09-09 · It’s not Easy

A correct earnings forecast can still lose money if the price assumed even better results. The Nifty Fifty show how high company quality can coexist with severe valuation risk. Write down what others appear to expect and the difference your evidence supports, then ask whether fees and errors consume that advantage. (pp. 2–7, 10–14) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2015-09-09-its-not-easy.pdf?sfvrsn=47bb0f65_7)

### 2015-10-22 · Inspiration from the World of Sports

A bettor’s edge depends on beating the spread. Marks uses fantasy football prices and the Seahawks’ intercepted Super Bowl pass to distinguish a sound decision from its observed result. Assess opportunities at their quoted odds and judge repeated choices against what could reasonably have been known beforehand. (pp. 3–9) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2015-10-22-inspiration-from-the-world-of-sports.pdf?sfvrsn=aa1f0e65_7)

## 2016

### 2016-01-14 · On the Couch

The 2015 market went from tolerating familiar problems to treating them as intolerable. Third Avenue’s redemption difficulty illustrates how fragile funding can force a sale even where value remains. Separate deterioration in economics from a change in investors’ attention, then increase buying selectively when prices allow for the risk. (pp. 4–7, 9–15) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/on-the-couch.pdf?sfvrsn=8a1e0165_7)

### 2016-01-19 · What Does the Market Know?

Leveraged selling drove senior loan prices down during the financial crisis, and falling prices triggered further margin calls. Price movements recorded urgent trades and collective opinion. Before treating a falling quote as fresh information, identify which cash flows changed and who had to sell. (pp. 2–6, 8) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/what-does-the-market-know.pdf?sfvrsn=cb7a0165_10)

### 2016-05-26 · Economic Reality

Policies alter the choices of workers, employers and buyers. Tariffs can protect one producer by raising its customers’ costs, while binding price controls can reduce supply. For a regulated business, trace the benefit to its payer and model substitution, entry, or withdrawal after people respond. (pp. 6–14) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/economic-reality.pdf?sfvrsn=49bc1865_6)

### 2016-08-17 · Political Reality

Efficiency gains and globalization can raise total output while concentrating persistent losses in particular occupations and regions. Marks links those losses and political incentives to support for Brexit and the U.S. outsider campaigns. For a policy-dependent business, identify organized losers as well as beneficiaries and ask how they can influence the rules. (pp. 7–15) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/political-reality.pdf?sfvrsn=39391265_2)

### 2016-11-07 · Implications of the Election

On the eve of the U.S. election, Marks treats voter anger and distrust as a demand that either winner would inherit. Electoral rules, party divisions and incentives to obstruct could frustrate responses. When modeling political change, trace the legislative coalition and incentives required to enact it. (pp. 2–11) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/implications-of-the-election_11716.pdf?sfvrsn=1389e965_4)

### 2016-11-14 · Go Figure!

Trump’s unexpected win was followed by a rally many had expected to follow a Clinton victory. Even a correct vote forecast needed a second, independent call on the market’s interpretation. Test an event thesis at three stages covering the outcome, implementable policy, and the surprise relative to price. (pp. 1–4, 6–8) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/go-figure.pdf?sfvrsn=ef38e965_7)

## 2017

### 2017-01-10 · Expert Opinion

Election pundits, football forecasters and macro managers supply different ways to test claims of foresight. A confident call on a favorite can be worthless once the odds reflect it. Seek a record against the appropriate baseline, and use macro uncertainty to stress exposures instead of relying on a single expert path. (pp. 2–10) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/expert-opinion.pdf?sfvrsn=ccffe365_4)

### 2017-04-18 · Lines in the Sand

A subscription credit line delays the date investors contribute cash. This can raise a fund’s annualized IRR even as interest expense reduces money earned, and it concentrates future capital calls. Reconstruct returns with immediate calls, compare cash multiples, and stress simultaneous calls across funds. (pp. 1–7) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/lines-in-the-sand.pdf?sfvrsn=bf5dfa65_2)

### 2017-07-26 · There They Go Again... Again

Argentina’s century bond, weak lending protections and enthusiasm for a handful of exceptional companies showed buyers accepting distant promises on meager terms. A low cash yield doesn’t enlarge the reward available on risky assets. Examine the underwriting terms others concede, and keep the option to pass. (pp. 5–13, 17–22) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/there-they-go-again-again.pdf?sfvrsn=56d4f265_6)

### 2017-09-07 · Yet Again?

Responding to readers who interpreted caution as a call to exit, Marks distinguishes security selection from adjusting portfolio risk. He reconsiders whether Bitcoin can serve as currency while questioning its valuation. Keep a valuation objection separate from a prediction of failure, and express uncertainty through position size and selectivity. (pp. 1–7, 9–11) [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/yet-again.pdf?sfvrsn=3767f765_6)
