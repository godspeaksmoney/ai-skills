# Howard Marks memo notes, 2018–2026

These notes follow Oaktree’s dated year listings through November 20, 2026. Each entry links to its individual source. Historical observations and the investment questions drawn from them reflect the setting in which the memo was written.

## 2018

### 2018-01-23 · Latest Thinking

Strengthening profits and a U.S. corporate tax cut improved near-term earnings while buyers were already paying high multiples. The removal of state-tax deductions could change where businesses and high earners locate, altering the tax base over time. When valuing a beneficiary of a policy change, separate a one-time earnings step-up from the rate at which those earnings can subsequently grow. Stronger fundamentals and a thinner margin of safety can coincide. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/latest-thinking.pdf?sfvrsn=a77ccf65_2)

### 2018-06-18 · Investing Without People

Index funds rely on active investors to establish the prices at which passive flows transact. An ETF can trade continuously even when selling the underlying bonds would require a steep discount. Data-driven strategies also face crowding when many managers recognize the same pattern. Test a platform’s liquidity where the underlying transaction settles. Look for judgment about qualitative, long-horizon outcomes that other investors cannot readily reproduce. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/investing-without-people.pdf?sfvrsn=7a5ec465_8)

### 2018-09-26 · The Seven Worst Words in the World

Competition among lenders can gradually transfer most of a deal’s economics to the borrower. Each winning bid may require a smaller spread, more leverage or less protection, especially when new funds must invest capital already raised. In direct lending, scrutinize a vintage’s underwriting terms and what happens at refinancing. A quiet period with few defaults reveals little about how loans will recover after a downturn. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/the-seven-worst-words-in-the-world.pdf?sfvrsn=6dc9dd65_4)

## 2019

### 2019-01-30 · Political Reality Meets Economic Reality

Tariffs on a raw material can help its producers while raising costs for domestic manufacturers that use it. Tax and benefit promises likewise change incentives, demand funding and can shift burdens beyond their intended recipients. For a policy-driven thesis, draw the entire chain from the immediate beneficiary to the customer, taxpayer or supplier bearing the cost. Test how those parties can alter their behavior. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/political-reality-meets-economic-reality.pdf?sfvrsn=21c1d665_8)

### 2019-04-01 · Growing the Pie

Marks contrasts China’s change in agricultural incentives with the debate over subsidies for Amazon’s proposed New York headquarters. Productivity and distribution can move independently. A community can gain aggregate income while disliking who receives it, and rejecting a concession can also forfeit jobs or investment. Estimate incremental benefits, alternatives and beneficiaries separately before interpreting the popular response as evidence of the project’s economics. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/growing-the-pie.pdf?sfvrsn=423fab65_8)

### 2019-06-12 · This Time It's Different

Examining claims about central banks, deferred recessions, deficit financing, inflation and the yield curve, Marks asks what would have to change for old constraints to cease applying. An expansion’s age alone says little about accumulated excess, and genuine technological change can alter a historical relationship. For a proposed structural break, record the mechanism and observations that would falsify it. Universal confidence in a permanently benign outcome is itself a warning. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/this-time-its-different.pdf?sfvrsn=fc19af65_10)

### 2019-07-26 · On the Other Hand

A rate cut can improve financing terms and simultaneously reveal that policymakers see weakness. It also transfers income between lenders and borrowers, can pull demand forward and leaves less policy room for the next shock. Before treating a cut as bullish, identify what the prior price anticipated, why authorities acted and whether the firm benefits more from cheaper financing than it suffers from the conditions behind the move. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/on-the-other-hand.pdf?sfvrsn=9b1a365_4)

### 2019-10-17 · Mysterious

Negative sovereign yields reverse familiar compounding arithmetic. A buyer may still value safety, expect deflation or currency appreciation, or anticipate an even more negative resale yield. Negative policy rates may induce households to save more to meet a fixed retirement goal. Stress both inflation-adjusted purchasing power and reinvestment risk. Moving into weaker credit to meet an unchanged nominal target introduces a different exposure. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/mysterious.pdf?sfvrsn=8a30a565_12)

## 2020

### 2020-01-13 · You Bet!

In backgammon, accepting a double can minimize expected loss even for a player who is behind. In investing, a favored outcome can make a poor wager at the offered odds, and an excellent wager can still lose. Record the range of payoffs and what the price implies. Then size the investment for survival, since even attractive odds cannot justify risking capital whose loss would prevent the next bet. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/you-bet.pdf?sfvrsn=785dbe65_8)

### 2020-03-03 · Nobody Knows II

At the outset of the pandemic, Marks distinguishes established facts, informed analogies and speculation. Disease transmission, responses by consumers, policy and market psychology had different uncertainties. A sharp fall could improve prospective returns while the economic outlook worsened. Compare the revised range of business values with the revised price and state what remains unknowable, including whether the decline will continue. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/nobody-knows-ii.pdf?sfvrsn=108eb165_8)

### 2020-03-19 · Latest Update

In the March sell-off, dealers’ retreat and holders’ urgent need for cash reduced liquidity and widened credit spreads. Comparison of high-yield indices with and without energy showed how one industry could distort the aggregate signal. Compare prices of individual claims with cash recoverable through a shutdown. Opportunity can emerge while sentiment is still deteriorating, so retain funds for further dislocations. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/weekly.pdf?sfvrsn=cbf3b065_8)

### 2020-03-31 · Which Way Now?

The quickest bear market was followed by a sharp rebound. Marks writes out credible positive and negative cases, including whether fiscal transfers merely replaced income lost during shutdown. Improving prices could reflect policy support, forced-flow reversal or an overly hopeful outlook for reopening. For each case, distinguish which obligations can be bridged with cash and which businesses may suffer lasting damage. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/which-way-now.pdf?sfvrsn=f5f4b765_8)

### 2020-04-06 · Calibrating

The choice facing investors was how much of a prior defensive tilt to unwind as prices fell. A bottom can be identified only after the rebound has begun. Define a normal portfolio risk level, then change exposure in increments as quoted terms and compensation improve. Keeping some capacity unused can be rational alongside buying today, because missing a recovery and exhausting cash too early are both costly. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/calibrating.pdf?sfvrsn=3e98b665_6)

### 2020-04-14 · Knowledge of the Future

Pandemic models were necessarily conditional on testing, behavior, restrictions and treatment, with policy responses themselves changing outcomes. A fiscal or central-bank rescue can keep firms alive while shifting credit losses and creating incentives for future risk-taking. Separate observations from model-dependent estimates and opinions. In a recovery case, specify who can resume activity, on what timetable, and who ultimately bears losses. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/knowledge-of-the-future.pdf?sfvrsn=fa39b665_6)

### 2020-05-11 · Uncertainty

The pandemic produced a long list of unanswered questions and an audience eager for certainty. Politically filtered information and the tendency to double down can make conviction stronger as evidence weakens. An unusual forecast creates an advantage only when it is both correct and absent from prices. Actively seek disconfirming evidence and set conditions under which the original investment thesis would be withdrawn. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/uncertainty.pdf?sfvrsn=d913b465_28)

### 2020-05-28 · Uncertainty II

Future outcomes depend on a chain of responses that can change after an initial forecast. Choosing which expert to trust may itself require expertise, and selecting someone who agrees with a prior view gives little independent confirmation. Ask what choices would redirect the path, how the expert’s record relates to this particular problem and what matters in the extreme tail of outcomes. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/uncertainty-ii.pdf?sfvrsn=d48a65_8)

### 2020-06-11 · Not Enough

After George Floyd’s killing, Marks argues that passive disapproval failed to address unequal access to education, employment, healthcare and security. He examines hiring, mentorship, training and charitable engagement as actions within the firm’s control. For organizational diligence, look for evidence that recruitment and advancement practices widened opportunity. The memo concerns civic and institutional responsibility and makes no claim about an expected investment premium. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/not-enough.pdf?sfvrsn=8f828c65_4)

### 2020-06-18 · The Anatomy of a Rally

Federal Reserve intervention, lower discount rates, expectations of recovery, index composition and renewed risk appetite offered several explanations for the rally from March lows. Explaining why a move occurred does not establish that the new price offers an adequate return. Translate the quote into assumptions about normalized earnings and risk premiums, then ask what disappointment a buyer would actually be paid to absorb. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/the-anatomy-of-a-rally.pdf?sfvrsn=f5828c65_4)

### 2020-08-05 · Time for Thinking

The shutdown put activity on hold by design. Economic support could sustain incomes during that pause even while an unresolved health shock threatened reopening. The technology leaders also represented a growing slice of the index, obscuring weakness elsewhere. Check the denominator and period on annualized growth figures, examine index concentration, and test the conditions required for underlying cash flows to recover. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/timeforthinking.pdf?sfvrsn=17818c65_8)

### 2020-10-13 · Coming into Focus

The disease was the trigger, the shutdown the transmission mechanism, and exceptional fiscal and monetary support the reason credit markets reopened so quickly. A rebound in financing and equity prices could coexist with failures among smaller employers. A low risk-free rate made many assets look reasonable relative to one another while leaving absolute prospective returns low. Underwrite company survival, sector dispersion and the withdrawal of support separately. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/coming-into-focus)

## 2021

### 2021-01-11 · Something of Value

Conversations with his son Andrew led Marks to revisit the growth/value divide. An expensive-looking multiple can reflect exceptionally durable reinvestment and network economics. A statistically cheap incumbent may be losing its franchise. Treat growth and intangible assets as inputs to intrinsic value. Specify why future cash flows could exceed consensus and how long the franchise can retain customers, earn attractive incremental returns and withstand disruption. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/something-of-value)

### 2021-03-04 · 2020 in Review

An abrupt collapse, a policy response and a rapid reopening of credit markets made the opportunity to buy public securities unusually brief. The strongest recovery scenario for the economy could also increase inflation or discount rates, weakening the case for already expensive securities. Evaluate starting valuation and the financing window alongside pent-up demand. The same macro development can help earnings and hurt the multiple. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/2020-in-review)

### 2021-07-29 · Thinking About Macro

Fiscal transfers and shortages made higher inflation plausible while normalization and disinflationary forces supported the opposite case. The equity and bond markets could appear to give conflicting signals about the same event. Distinguish exposure to an inflation shock from confidence that one can forecast it. Test how pricing, wages, financing and the valuation multiple would behave under both persistent and transitory inflation. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/thinking-about-macro)

### 2021-11-23 · The Winds of Change

Information-based businesses can expand with modest extra capital or labor, changing the link between output, corporate earnings and employment. Automation and flexible work can create opportunity while displacing workers whose skills or location no longer match jobs. For a growth company, test the incremental cost of serving another customer, who captures the productivity gain, and whether the social or political response threatens that advantage. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/the-winds-of-change)

## 2022

### 2022-01-13 · Selling Out

A gain, a loss or an enlarged portfolio weight can trigger a sale for emotional reasons. Selling a long-term compounder also creates a reinvestment decision and can impose tax costs. Reassess future return from today’s quote, alternative uses of capital, concentration and changes in the business. Decide in advance what deterioration or opportunity cost would make selling justified. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/selling-out)

### 2022-03-23 · The Pendulum in International Affairs

Europe’s dependence on Russian energy and the concentration of semiconductor supply illustrate the cost of optimizing a supply chain for price and efficiency alone. Geopolitical disruption can move priorities toward secure supply, redundancy and domestic production, with higher costs and potential openings for alternative suppliers. Identify the critical input or counterparty, the time to replace it and who can pass through the cost of resilience. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/the-pendulum-in-international-affairs)

### 2022-05-26 · Bull Market Rhymes

In the 2020–21 boom, successful technology businesses, speculative listings and meme stocks attracted buyers for different reasons. Easy capital spread across them as gains themselves became evidence for buying, and as lenders relaxed terms to win business. Monitor the quality of new issues and concessions made at the margin. After the reversal, compare each company’s price with its own earning power, since a large fall alone proves little. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/bull-market-rhymes)

### 2022-06-23 · Conversation at Panmure House

In a discussion at Adam Smith’s former home, Marks explains why prices fluctuate far more than underlying output and why an efficiency assumption can be a useful starting hurdle for an active investor. Psychology, leverage and forced selling can reinforce one another. Assess sentiment using several imperfect indicators such as deal terms and ease of raising money. A composite reading is a judgment and cannot reliably date a turn. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/conversation-at-panmure-house)

### 2022-07-26 · I Beg to Differ

A non-consensus position can produce exceptional gains or losses. The Yale endowment example combines a differentiated allocation with the institutional ability to endure years of looking wrong. Write down where consensus is reflected in the price, what it overlooks, why it may be reasonable, and why the alternative is better grounded. Identify the capital or governance constraint that might force abandonment before the thesis resolves. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/i-beg-to-differ)

### 2022-09-08 · The Illusion of Knowledge

Macro forecasting requires a valid model, correct inputs and predictable interactions among people who can change their behavior. Even a broadly accurate prediction offers little edge if others have already discounted it. Evaluate a forecaster’s whole record, especially the unconventional calls that actually changed outcomes. Use uncertainty about macro variables to test resilience and concentrate research where a business-specific judgment can be checked. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/the-illusion-of-knowledge)

### 2022-11-22 · What Really Matters?

Long-horizon holders can tolerate mark-to-market losses when financing and spending needs allow them to hold. Infrequent private valuations can make volatility appear low while leaving economic risks intact. Ask what the company can earn, whether today’s quote compensates for loss and whether an investor can hold through adversity. Judge skill by upside participation and protection across a cycle, with realistic benchmarks and net costs. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/what-really-matters)

### 2022-12-13 · Sea Change

Falling interest rates over four decades supported asset values, leverage and refinancing. By late 2022, higher rates had changed the return available from lending, while raising the hurdle for asset owners who had depended on easy funding. Rebuild expected returns from the current yield, spread, purchase multiple and financing terms. A historical track record can embed a nonrepeatable tailwind. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/sea-change)

## 2023

### 2023-04-17 · Lessons from Silicon Valley Bank

Silicon Valley Bank paired long-duration securities with concentrated, runnable deposits. A rate increase reduced the market value of its assets. Withdrawals forced the bank to face losses it could otherwise have carried, and communication among connected depositors accelerated the run. Stress liquidity and accounting values together. Read actual capital-instrument terms and regulatory discretion before assuming that a familiar label fixes recovery priority. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/lessons-from-silicon-valley-bank)

### 2023-07-10 · Taking the Temperature

Marks revisits five calls selected because major extremes were visible and the calls worked. Their evidence came from behavior, such as disappearing underwriting discipline in the credit boom or investors selling indiscriminately in panic. Use such observations to adjust the degree of risk taken, without relying on a target index level or an exact turn date. The selected successes are illustrative rather than a measured hit rate. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/taking-the-temperature)

### 2023-09-12 · Fewer Losers, or More Winners?

Performing loans have limited upside, which makes avoiding a few substantial defaults more valuable than seeking modest extra yield on each winner. Equity returns can depend on retaining a small number of outsized winners despite many failures. Choose a strategy whose payoff distribution fits the investor’s skill, tolerance and mandate. Measure the size of winners and losers as well as their frequency. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/fewer-losers-or-more-winners)

### 2023-10-11 · Further Thoughts on Sea Change

First sent to clients on May 30, this October release compares then-available contractual credit yields with a long-term institutional spending requirement. An endowment might reach its goal with less uncertainty when reasonably underwritten credit offers sufficient yield. Deduct defaults and expenses, consider forced-sale and reinvestment risk and retain equity upside where useful. The rates quoted describe the original May opportunity. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/further-thoughts-on-sea-change)

## 2024

### 2024-01-09 · Easy Money

Persistently cheap financing can raise asset prices, encourage leverage, conceal weak underwriting and keep otherwise uneconomic projects alive. Each phase changes the conditions for the next cycle as apparent success encourages investors to accept still less compensation. Re-estimate a deal at a sustainable borrowing cost and with tighter terms. Attribute historic gains separately to operating progress, multiple expansion and financial leverage. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/easy-money)

### 2024-04-17 · The Indispensability of Risk

A chess player can exchange material for an uncertain positional advantage. The investment parallel is sacrificing certainty for a favorable set of possibilities. Avoiding all uncertainty can itself make a spending or return target unreachable. Make the risk concrete, identify the benefit purchased by accepting it, and ask whether the price and position size permit enough adverse outcomes to keep investing. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/the-indispensability-of-risk)

### 2024-05-08 · The Impact of Debt

Debt magnifies equity outcomes and can interrupt the time required for a good investment to recover. A covenant, margin call or short funding maturity can turn a temporary fluctuation into an irrevocable sale. Size debt against a plausible extreme and the terms on which lenders can act. A long-lived business’s financing choice should preserve its ability to continue operating when refinancing is unavailable. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/the-impact-of-debt)

### 2024-07-17 · The Folly of Certainty

Correctly forecasting an election or a central-bank decision still leaves the market response uncertain. Investors can be right about one projection and lose on the trade built around it. Compare several combinations of the event and the response, including favorable outcomes for the firm that disappoint the expectations embedded in its shares. Express confidence in proportion to evidence and retain room to revise the position. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/the-folly-of-certainty)

### 2024-08-22 · Mr. Market Miscalculates

The yen carry-trade unwind illustrates how leverage, simultaneous selling and attention to one side of the news can generate large cross-market moves. An observer inferring fundamentals from that movement can accidentally amplify it. Compare the move with changes in cash-flow estimates and funding conditions. Investigate who had to transact before accepting the quote as evidence that others know more. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/mr-market-miscalculates)

### 2024-09-19 · Shall We Repeal the Laws of Economics?

Anti-gouging proposals and rent controls can protect incumbent buyers but change whether suppliers offer additional capacity, maintain buildings or serve peak demand. Model the immediate transfer as well as the later response in quantity, investment and availability. For a regulated company, trace how the rule changes incentives for the firm, competing providers and customers over time. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/shall-we-repeal-the-laws-of-economics)

### 2024-10-22 · Ruminating on Asset Allocation

The basic portfolio choice lies between owning assets with open-ended payoffs and lending against contractual terms with capped upside. These create different distributions of outcomes. Start with the investor’s need for loss protection and growth, then choose the blend of claims and their prices. Count leverage and look-through exposures before assuming that several named asset classes provide diversification. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/ruminating-on-asset-allocation)

## 2025

### 2025-01-07 · On Bubble Watch

The Nifty Fifty and dot-com episodes show how excellent businesses can be extremely poor purchases when shareholders assume durable dominance at any price. Concentration and high multiples are warning signs, while euphoria, fear of exclusion and disregard for valuation are evidence about psychology. Estimate what growth and duration justify the quote. Check the historical turnover among corporate leaders before assuming today’s winners will dominate for decades. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/on-bubble-watch)

### 2025-03-06 · Gimme Credit

Credit spread measures the extra yield over a comparable government bond. The investor actually receives a contractual stream exposed to defaults, recovery and reinvestment. A tighter spread can coexist with an attractive absolute yield and a better-quality issuer mix. Compare credit and equity on forward, loss-adjusted terms. In private lending, include leverage, exit constraints and the possibility of a stale mark when deciding whether the additional yield pays. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/gimme-credit)

### 2025-04-09 · Nobody Knows (Yet Again)

The April tariff announcement changed possible trading arrangements faster than forecasting models could absorb. Importers initially pay the levy, but consumers, foreign suppliers and downstream manufacturers may share the incidence. Retaliation, exchange rates and investment delays create further paths. Reprice the distribution of company outcomes and compare it with the market’s reaction. A larger credit spread can supply compensation while the underlying likelihood of distress increases. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/nobody-knows-yet-again)

### 2025-06-18 · More on Repealing the Laws of Economics

Insurance price caps can prompt providers to stop writing policies where expected claims exceed premiums. Rent controls and tariffs similarly change how much is supplied and which consumers ultimately pay. Stated trade objectives can conflict if a measure intended to reduce imports is also expected to provide durable tax revenue. Identify the binding constraint, the party free to withdraw and the costs that move off the initial bill. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/more-on-repealing-the-laws-of-economics)

### 2025-08-14 · The Calculus of Value

Value depends on the earnings an asset can distribute over time, whereas the quote can move sharply with mood. A different mix of asset-light, fast-growing businesses may warrant a higher index multiple, though a better mix alone cannot justify any price. Estimate prospective return from current earnings, growth and the eventual valuation. When price runs ahead of plausible value, exposure can be trimmed progressively. The archive posts this on August 14. The memo is signed August 13. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/the-calculus-of-value)

### 2025-10-12 · The Best of . . .

Marks’s signed 35th-anniversary preface identifies two reasons for writing the memos. One is to explain recurrent investment problems such as risk, cycles and disagreements with consensus. The other is to record how large events looked while they were developing. In a case study, pair the general principle with contemporaneous observations and date both. This entry covers only the signed preface of the dated individual page. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/the-best-of)

### 2025-10-28 · A Look Under the Hood

A state pension board exercise separates willingness to take risk from capacity to absorb it, and payment obligations from the temptation to beat peers. A seemingly stable private loan and a marked bond can share the same borrower default risk. Define the institution’s cash needs first, then assess risk, manager selection and illiquidity against its purpose. Evaluate results in the market conditions and risk posture the board actually authorized. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/a-look-under-the-hood)

### 2025-11-06 · Cockroaches in the Coal Mine

First Brands and Tricolor illustrate why individual credit failures warrant investigation before inferring contagion. An acquisition-heavy organization, opaque controls and questions about collateral can matter more than its headline margin. Easy funding can allow problems to accumulate unseen. Verify ownership and prior pledges of collateral and reconcile reported earnings to cash. Test common exposures, leverage and counterparty links before calling a failure systemic. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/cockroaches-in-the-coal-mine)

### 2025-12-09 · Is It a Bubble?

A transformative technology can create infrastructure valuable to society while leaving the investors who financed it with poor returns. In the AI build-out, competitors can lower prices, users can retain productivity gains, and debt on speculative capacity can survive longer than the hardware’s economic life. Compare projected utilization, contract duration and margins with funding commitments. Identify which layer of the industry has a defensible claim on the value created. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/is-it-a-bubble)

## 2026

### 2026-02-26 · AI Hurtles Ahead

Hands-on work with an AI model changed Marks’s assessment of its capacity for structured analysis. Deployment for inference responds to observable usage, whereas some training and infrastructure spending relies on demand still to come. Capability, profitable adoption and attractive security prices require separate judgments. Test what customers will pay after competition and compute costs, and distinguish verifiable outputs from areas requiring judgment about counterparties or unfamiliar risks. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/ai-hurtles-ahead)

### 2026-04-09 · What’s Going on in Private Credit?

Success drew capital into direct lending, encouraged looser terms and exposed some portfolios to the same recurring-revenue software borrowers. AI could threaten those cash flows just as higher rates strain interest cover, exit values and refinancings. Retail fund redemption limits have their own economics. Analyse loan impairment, seniority and stressed enterprise value separately from investors’ ability to redeem at the reported net asset value. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/whats-going-on-in-private-credit)

### 2026-09-22 · Shall We Repeal the Laws of Economics – Part III

Treasury purchases of long bonds may move the observed yield temporarily, but deficits, demand for funds and investors’ inflation expectations determine what buyers will accept over time. A sovereign may repay in full in nominal currency while delivering less purchasing power. Include refinancing costs and real returns when examining long-duration claims. A debt problem can be identifiable without making either its resolution date or a profitable exit trade predictable. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/shall-we-repeal-the-laws-of-economics---part-iii)

### 2026-11-11 · A Layman Thinks About AI

AI investing can require simultaneous judgments about technical progress, competition, pricing, utilization, financing and the life of costly infrastructure. A frontier-model shareholder and a lender against a leased data center face different failure conditions even though both hold AI exposure. Identify which assumptions must hold for the specific claim, which can be independently verified and which risks repeat elsewhere in the portfolio. Size the exposure for what remains unknowable. [Individual memo](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/insights/memo/a-layman-thinks-about-ai)
