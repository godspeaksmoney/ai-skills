# Howard Marks memo studies, 1990–2006

50 individual Oaktree memos across 17 annual index sections. Page references use the individual PDF page numbers. The dates below are the dates printed by Oaktree.

## 1990

### 1990-10-12 — The Route to Performance

A pension plan reached the fourth percentile over the long run while usually finishing between the 27th and 47th percentiles in individual years. Avoiding a catastrophic year did more for compounding than alternating between first and last place. In a credit analysis, look for recoverable principal in the weak cases before increasing the coupon assumption. In a portfolio, evaluate how often an error can wipe out earlier gains. [Original memo, pp. 1–2](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1990-10-12-the-route-to-performance.pdf?sfvrsn=33bc0f65_6)

## 1991

### 1991-04-11 — First Quarter Performance

Recession and Gulf uncertainty depressed lower-rated securities late in 1990. Their first-quarter rebound required little improvement in the economy because buyers had already assumed so much bad news. Ask how much of a distress discount remains after a rally. Buying heavily at the old price and buying heavily at the new one are different decisions even with the same view of the issuer. [Original memo, pp. 1–2](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1991-04-11-first-quarter-client-performance.pdf?sfvrsn=d7bd0f65_6)

## 1992

### 1992-10-08 — Microeconomics 101: Supply, Demand and Convertibles

Convertibles illustrate how capital supply can overwhelm the merits of a security. Heavy subscriptions before 1987 forced funds to buy expensive paper. Subsequent withdrawals reduced competition and improved entry terms for a bond with equity upside. Compare fund flows with the opportunity set and inspect conversion terms and bond protection afresh. The structure alone promises no particular return. [Original memo, pp. 1–2](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1992-10-08-microeconomics.pdf?sfvrsn=43bc0f65_6)

## 1993

### 1993-02-15 — The Value of Predictions, or Where'd All This Rain Come From?

A correct consensus forecast generally earns the return already embedded in prices. To earn excess returns from a macro call, a manager must differ, be right, implement in time, and overcome trading costs. The odds deteriorate when an investment needs an economic forecast, a rate response, a market response, and the correct stock. Record the necessary chain and judge forecasts against the information available when made. [Original memo, pp. 1–7](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1993-02-15-the-value-of-predictions-or-where-39-d-all-this-rain-come-from.pdf?sfvrsn=6fbc0f65_6)

## 1994

### 1994-01-24 — Random Thoughts on the Identification of Investment Opportunities

An auction with one or two willing bidders is a better starting point for a bargain than one with a queue. Fear can screen high-yield issuance by forcing borrowers to meet exacting terms. In an optimistic market even weak credits can be sold. Before calling an unpopular asset cheap, identify the prospective payout, the people unwilling to bid, and the price that compensates a buyer for accepting their concern. [Original memo, pp. 1–2](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1994-01-24-random-thoughts-on-the-identification-of-inv-opportunities.pdf?sfvrsn=17bc0f65_6)

### 1994-02-17 — Risk in Today's Markets

Low cash yields and three years of rewards for risk-taking encouraged investors to extend maturities, lower credit quality, borrow, or move into unfamiliar markets. Each choice looked like a small step from the previous mandate. Together they materially changed the portfolio. Reconstruct the exposure behind headline gains and test it in an environment that reverses the conditions which rewarded it. [Original memo, pp. 1–4](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1994-02-17-risk-in-todays-markets.pdf?sfvrsn=dfbd0f65_6)

### 1994-04-11 — "Risk in Today's Markets" Revisited

A modest Fed tightening produced a broad selloff as optimism broke, competing bond yields rose and leveraged investors unwound positions across markets. Fees that paid managers a share of gains could leave clients below their starting wealth even if the underlying strategy subsequently broke even. Examine manager incentives, leverage and forced-sale channels together, especially when past returns were earned at a smaller fund size. [Original memo, pp. 1–5](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1994-04-11-risk-in-todays-markets-revisted.pdf?sfvrsn=2bbc0f65_6)

### 1994-07-15 — "How Does an Inefficient Market Get That Way?"

Market efficiency needs participants willing and able to trade on both sides. Institutional reluctance to own high-yield debt survived both attractive risk premiums and the availability of public information. A pricing discrepancy can persist when career incentives, mandates or stigma prevent informed capital from arbitraging it. Identify an actual constraint on the other side before assuming an unpopular sector offers an edge. [Original memo, pp. 1–2](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1994-07-15-how-does-an-inefficient-market-get-that-way.pdf?sfvrsn=dbbd0f65_6)

## 1995

### 1995-05-26 — How the Game Should Be Played

The newly founded Oaktree used careers in golf and baseball to explain how steady high placement can beat occasional championships interspersed with poor seasons. An enduring batting average also changes which ideas a manager is willing to pursue. Evaluate each opportunity by its contribution to repeated results through a full cycle, including what a serious miss would cost. [Original memo, pp. 1–2](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1995-05-26-how-the-game-should-be-played.pdf?sfvrsn=d3bd0f65_6)

## 1996

### 1996-07-22 — The Value of Predictions II (or "Give That Man a Cigar")

In a small series of Wall Street Journal surveys, expert forecasts of rates and exchange rates closely resembled prevailing levels. Prior winners did poorly when the direction changed, while a consistently extreme forecaster could win one round by construction. Compare each forecast with a simple unchanged-level baseline, publish misses alongside successes, and distinguish repeatability from winning a single contest. [Original memo, pp. 1–5](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1996-07-22-the-value-of-predictions-ii-or-give-that-man-a-cigar.pdf?sfvrsn=27bc0f65_8)

### 1996-11-25 — Will It Be Different This Time?

Contemporary arguments that recessions had been tamed echoed arguments made before earlier downturns. The evidence for confidence can be genuine even as a foundation switching from a long-held bond allocation to stocks late in a boom signals capitulation. Distinguish an improvement in businesses from a permanent abolition of cyclicality, and ask which expectation today’s price requires. [Original memo, pp. 1–4](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1996-11-25-will-it-be-different-this-time.pdf?sfvrsn=23bc0f65_6)

## 1997

### 1997-09-03 — Are You An Investor or a Speculator?

The same commercial paper attracted a money-market buyer at par and a distressed buyer at 77 cents after a scandal. At par, a small coupon offered scant compensation for hidden credit exposure. At the discount, several resolution paths could produce a gain despite obvious trouble. Write down the payout by claim and entry price before deciding which buyer was taking the greater risk. [Original memo, pp. 1–5](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/are-you-an-investor-or-a-speculator.pdf?sfvrsn=1e37cf65_2)

## 1998

### 1998-01-08 — Who Knew?

The Asian crisis exposed risks few participants had foreseen. The combination of unresolved questions, elevated prices and a confident market mattered more than identifying the next shock. Late in a bull market, good news can be largely owned already. Ask what improvement markets still need and what happens to price if optimism merely stops increasing. [Original memo, pp. 1–3](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1998-01-08-who-knew.pdf?sfvrsn=2fbc0f65_6)

### 1998-10-09 — Genius Isn't Enough (and Other Lessons from Long-Term Capital Management)

LTCM borrowed heavily against small relative-value spreads. Its long and short securities were related but distinct, and their prices diverged as credit tightened. Later convergence offered little help to a holder forced out early. Stress the basis, financing terms, liquidity and common creditors together, and check whether the trade can be held long enough for its economics to prevail. [Original memo, pp. 2–7](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1998-10-09-genius-isn-39-t-enough.pdf?sfvrsn=eac20f65_6)

## 1999

### 1999-04-15 — How's the Market?

A record index day concealed a narrow market. Priceline’s valuation and the very different reception for an ordinary technology-services firm illustrated the premium paid for being in vogue. Employment news could support either a bull or a bear narrative depending on sentiment. Check market breadth, valuation and the reception of comparable companies before accepting a headline as evidence of widespread strength. [Original memo, pp. 1–4](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/1999-04-15-hows-the-market.pdf?sfvrsn=3fbc0f65_6)

## 2000

### 2000-01-02 — bubble.com

Internet adoption and incumbent disruption could be real while dot-com shareholders earned poor returns. Webvan’s order economics and the relative valuations of travel intermediaries and airlines raised concrete profitability questions. Venture returns also depended on receptive IPO buyers. Start with unit contribution, competition and cash requirements, then identify the profit eventually required by the share price. [Original memo, pp. 3–12](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2000-01-02-bubble.pdf?sfvrsn=37bc0f65_5)

### 2000-05-01 — Irrational Exuberance

Latecomers bought rising technology stocks, then bought the dips, then fled when momentum broke. Several stocks fell much further than the broad market. Widely distributed information can enter prices quickly while an irrational consensus still assigns the wrong value. Test whether the owner would keep the holding if peers stopped buying, and which cash flows would justify doing so. [Original memo, pp. 4–9](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2000-05-01-irrational-exuberance.pdf?sfvrsn=cfbc0f65_6)

### 2000-11-16 — Investment Miscellany

A consultant’s equity-return assumption, fire-sale liquidity and the false Emulex press release exposed different ways that prices and fundamental knowledge diverge. An urgent seller can move several otherwise unrelated assets together. Rapid reaction to false information demonstrates speed, not accuracy. Separate organic cash generation, trading pressure and new verified facts when explaining a move. [Original memo, pp. 2–7](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2000-11-16-investment-miscellany.pdf?sfvrsn=1fbc0f65_6)

### 2000-12-31 — We're Not In 1999 Anymore, Toto

The disappearance of first-day IPO gains cut off exit liquidity for venture capital and reversed a reinforcing loop. Markets could now dismiss every telecom business as indiscriminately as they had once embraced them. Distinguish companies with financing and viable operations from those dependent on a reopening of the market. Review the holding period required even if a long-run thesis survives. [Original memo, pp. 2–9](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2000-12-31-were-not-in-1999-anymore.pdf?sfvrsn=3bbc0f65_6)

## 2001

### 2001-04-10 — Safety First . . . But Where?

Index membership, a blue-chip name, high-grade bonds, the Fed and a hedge-fund label each offered forms of reassurance that could prove expensive. An index adding yesterday’s expensive winners can institutionalize performance chasing. For a defensive holding, identify its actual source of downside protection and the conditions in which it disappears. For a fund, test capacity, fees and downside behavior. [Original memo, pp. 2–10](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2001-04-10-safety-first-but-where.pdf?sfvrsn=49c10f65_8)

### 2001-07-11 — What's It All About, Alpha?

A less efficient market creates more opportunities for both skill and mistakes. Wide dispersion of manager returns creates a selection problem for the allocator. Alpha can appear as similar returns with less downside. Evaluate a manager against a relevant exposure across contrasting markets, consider how the holding interacts with the rest of the portfolio, and define loss in terms of the owner’s needs and horizon. [Original memo, pp. 3–9](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2001-07-11-whats-it-all-about-alpha.pdf?sfvrsn=13bc0f65_6)

### 2001-09-16 — Notes from New York

Marks’s personal letter after September 11 records the loss of a sense of safety, the courage of emergency workers and ordinary people, and hard trade-offs between safety and liberties. Family and human relationships were his central concern. Preserve this letter as evidence about the experience of unprecedented shocks. Its author did not use it to make a market call. [Original memo, pp. 1–6](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2001-09-16-notes-from-ny.pdf?sfvrsn=fbc0f65_6)

### 2001-10-04 — What Lies Ahead?

After September 11 the range of plausible outcomes widened. Damaged confidence could affect spending and create a real economic feedback loop, but neither its duration nor the market reaction could be known. Use normality as a working case, explicitly widen adverse cases and retain enough protection to invest through them. An exclusively catastrophic assumption can itself remove the ability to participate. [Original memo, pp. 2–7](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2001-10-04-what-lies-ahead.pdf?sfvrsn=bbc0f65_6)

### 2001-11-20 — You Can't Predict. You Can Prepare.

A prosperous economy grows lenders’ capital and confidence. Competition then lowers underwriting standards, finances weak projects and produces the defaults that cause credit to contract. A sound company can fail at the trough if it needs refinancing then. Stress the sequence of cash needs and combine economic, financing, product and valuation cycles when they can work against the same company. [Original memo, pp. 2–11](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2001-11-20-you-cant-predict-you-can-prepare.pdf?sfvrsn=bc00f65_6)

## 2002

### 2002-03-14 — Learning From Enron

Enron converted uncertain future contract values into current earnings and used special-purpose entities to obscure financing risk. Incentives affected executives, directors, auditors and analysts, so apparent corroboration could come from people with aligned commercial interests. Reconcile reported earnings to collectible cash and guarantees, and check who negotiated each related-party transaction and who could challenge its valuation. [Original memo, pp. 2–20](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2002-03-14-learning-from-enron.pdf?sfvrsn=77bc0f65_6)

### 2002-05-31 — The Realist's Creed

Marks set out a defensive operating philosophy and then tested it against equities and hedge funds. Holding period can make historical average returns irrelevant to the investor who must withdraw early. Alternative funds can gather enough assets and charge enough fees to erode their advantage. Declare the horizon and loss tolerance first, then justify each source of expected return at the available price. [Original memo, pp. 1–11](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2002-05-31-the-realists-creed.pdf?sfvrsn=1bbc0f65_5)

### 2002-07-26 — Quo Vadis?

Following the equity collapse, past post-recession patterns and heavy selling supported a recovery argument, while reduced confidence, earnings pressure and governance failures supported caution. A large decline from an extraordinary peak can leave ordinary valuations. Revalue cash flows and assess actual risk premiums after a fall. The percentage off the high and a collection of plausible narratives cannot locate a bottom. [Original memo, pp. 1–8](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2002-07-26-quo-vadis.pdf?sfvrsn=67bc0f65_6)

### 2002-09-04 — Etorre's Wisdom

A seemingly fast highway lane attracts drivers until the advantage disappears. Chasing recently successful managers or styles can change the opportunity in much the same way. The apparent winner’s record may also have been earned when the fund was much smaller. Identify why other capital has stayed away, how entry affects the price, and whether the edge survives a large allocation. [Original memo, pp. 1–5](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2002-09-04-etorres-wisdom.pdf?sfvrsn=63bc0f65_6)

### 2002-11-11 — Returns and How They Get That Way

Equity returns can come from dividends, retained earnings used productively and changes in valuation. Separate these from general market exposure, manager skill and luck. A strategy with spectacular upside and similarly enlarged downside may chiefly add market sensitivity. Compare results in different environments and consider alternative outcomes that the decision could reasonably have produced at the time. [Original memo, pp. 1–14](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2002-11-11-returns-and-how-they-get-that-way.pdf?sfvrsn=4fbc0f65_8)

## 2003

### 2003-03-11 — Whad’Ya Know?

Shares always have both a buyer and a seller. Cash on the sidelines matters only if willingness to pay changes. Commentators could freely reverse the explanation of a day’s move, and calendar patterns or ratings offered little predictive proof. Demand a mechanism, a competing explanation and a track record before treating a market statistic as a decision input. [Original memo, pp. 1–14](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2003-03-11-whad-ya-know.pdf?sfvrsn=abbc0f65_6)

### 2003-05-06 — What's Going On?

The collapse in willingness to own distressed bonds in 2002 enabled purchases at exceptionally low prices and a sharp ensuing rally. Fresh buyers then competed for a small opportunity set and eroded future returns. Opportunity-rich yesterday and crowded today can both be accurate. Reassess entry terms after the rally and limit deployment when assets available at an adequate spread are scarce. [Original memo, pp. 3–8](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2003-05-16-whats-going-on.pdf?sfvrsn=73bc0f65_6)

### 2003-07-01 — The Most Important Thing

Valuation, conviction, risk control, specialization and firm culture work together. A falling price can invite buying only if the value estimate is defensible. Strong conviction in an erroneous valuation magnifies damage. Other constraints include the amount of capital a strategy can deploy and what its clients expect. State which premise must be true, how it could fail and how large a loss the position permits. [Original memo, pp. 1–13](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2003-07-01-the-most-important-thing.pdf?sfvrsn=91c00f65_6)

### 2003-09-05 — What’s Your Game Plan?

Investment careers often end after concentrated or leveraged losses. The same portfolio must supply offense and defense throughout changing conditions, like soccer players doing both jobs. Investors also have the freedom to pass on pitches. Choose an approach the team can execute repeatedly, and tie the amount of capital raised or invested to the supply of opportunities it can genuinely underwrite. [Original memo, pp. 1–7](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2003-09-05-whats-your-game-plan.pdf?sfvrsn=7bbc0f65_6)

### 2003-10-02 — The Feeling's Mutual

Stale overseas closes and orders accepted after a fund’s cutoff let advantaged traders profit at the expense of existing holders. Managers could benefit by attracting fee-paying assets even when continuing shareholders lost. Inspect pricing rules, fee arrangements and board independence. Ask who receives the incremental revenue, who bears dilution and who has both power and incentive to intervene. [Original memo, pp. 1–8](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2003-10-02-the-feelings-mutual.pdf?sfvrsn=47bc0f65_6)

## 2004

### 2004-03-16 — Hey, Steward!!

Revenue sharing, directed brokerage and distribution charges could reward intermediaries for selling products that served customers poorly. Fine print might technically disclose a conflict while leaving its significance opaque. Translate each arrangement into money paid, recipient and beneficiary. The willingness to explain the deal plainly on the front page is a practical check on a fiduciary’s conduct. [Original memo, pp. 2–10](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2004-03-16-hey-steward!!.pdf?sfvrsn=a7bc0f65_6)

### 2004-05-07 — Us and Them

An investor’s stance toward what is knowable affects concentration, turnover, capacity and willingness to hold cash. Buffett provided examples of acting on specific value, accepting idle capital and recognizing that size reduces returns. Look at behavior when compelling investments are missing. A willingness to do less in those conditions is evidence about the stated philosophy. [Original memo, pp. 1–8](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2004-05-07-us-and-them.pdf?sfvrsn=bfbc0f65_6)

### 2004-07-20 — The Happy Medium

Small economic changes can be amplified by operating and financial leverage, then by credit availability and the willingness of investors to pay for future profits. Urgent buyers can create distortions just as urgent sellers do. Draw the links from demand to operating earnings to the multiple, and stress all three together before treating a long-run average return as a likely annual result. [Original memo, pp. 1–10](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2004-07-21-the-happy-medium.pdf?sfvrsn=4bbc0f65_6)

### 2004-10-06 — Hedge Funds: A Case for Caution

A hedge fund is a vehicle with pricing terms, not a homogeneous trade. Capital chasing a limited number of mispricings reduces spreads, pushes managers toward leverage and brings less experienced operators into the sample. Distinguish net skill from a leveraged directional return. Test whether the old record could have been earned at today’s fund size and across an adverse cycle. [Original memo, pp. 3–13](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2004-10-06-hedge-funds-a-case-for-caution.pdf?sfvrsn=57bc0f65_6)

### 2004-10-27 — Risk and Return Today

Low short rates reduced the starting point for investment returns, while diminished risk aversion also compressed the compensation for moving out the risk spectrum. Both the level and slope changed. Compare each alternative’s incremental expected payoff with the added loss exposure and the yield on safer assets. A high target return cannot repair a flat menu of available opportunities. [Original memo, pp. 1–7](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2004-10-27-risk-and-return-today.pdf?sfvrsn=53bc0f65_6)

## 2005

### 2005-04-11 — Oaktree at Ten

Oaktree’s ten-year retrospective associated performance with a stable team, shared ownership and specialist expansion around existing capabilities. Growth in assets was one result. The stated business priorities placed client outcomes first. In assessing a manager, ask whether team incentives and product expansion preserve the conditions that produced the earlier record and who owns the resulting economics. [Original memo, pp. 1–5](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2005-04-11-oaktree-at-ten.pdf?sfvrsn=5fbc0f65_6)

### 2005-05-06 — There They Go Again

Housing scarcity arguments ignored the response of construction and the cost of the purchase price. Across credit and buyouts, a lower underlying rate combined with narrower spreads and much more money looking for deals. Separate asset economics from cheap financing and from appreciation. If the combination leaves little prospective return, model cash or lower target returns before taking extra risk. [Original memo, pp. 2–9](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2005-05-06-there-they-go-again.pdf?sfvrsn=5bbc0f65_6)

### 2005-06-06 — A Case in Point

A convertible arbitrage position combined a long convertible with a partial equity short. In GM, an equity bid lifted the shares while a downgrade hurt the debt, so both legs could lose at once. Crowded funds then faced common exits and redemptions. Stress the individual legs under separate news events and estimate the liquidity available if peers also unwind. [Original memo, pp. 1–6](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2005-06-06-a-case-in-point.pdf?sfvrsn=a3bc0f65_6)

### 2005-10-17 — Hindsight First, Please (or, What Were They Thinking?)

Bayou’s conflicts, questionable trading credentials and a purported auditor tied to its own CFO were warning signs available before the collapse. In lending, aggressive competition similarly showed up in terms long before losses appeared. Reconstruct the facts a future postmortem would call obvious. Verify counterparties and independence directly and ask what default or financing conditions current terms implicitly assume. [Original memo, pp. 1–12](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2005-10-17-hindsight-first-please-or-what-where-they-thinking.pdf?sfvrsn=93bc0f65_6)

## 2006

### 2006-01-19 — Risk

Riskier assets need to offer a sufficiently attractive prospective return to draw capital, yet their range of outcomes widens and includes larger losses. A benign outcome reveals little about risk actually taken. Match each loss scenario to the owner’s objective, liquidity needs, cross-holdings and capacity to absorb it. Then judge whether the price pays enough for accepting it. [Original memo, pp. 2–12](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2006-01-19-risk.pdf?sfvrsn=afbc0f65_6)

### 2006-03-27 — It Is What It Is

Marks’s market checklist contrasts willing sellers, restrictive lending and scarce funds with eager buyers, permissive funds and easy fundraising. None times a turning point, but together they help set how hard to press an opportunity. Scale commitments and return assumptions to the conditions actually observable. An investor’s spending requirement cannot improve the menu of opportunities. [Original memo, pp. 2–7](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2006-03-27-it-is-what-it-is.pdf?sfvrsn=87bc0f65_6)

### 2006-06-13 — Returns, Absolute Returns and Risk

Claims of absolute returns and portable alpha deserve a decomposition. Pairing a manager with index exposure through futures can restore market risk and add leverage if the supposed alpha fund also carries beta. Assess performance across rising and falling markets, after costs. Comparable returns earned with less permanent-loss exposure can represent as much skill as higher returns at the same risk. [Original memo, pp. 2–12](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2006-06-13-returns-absolute-returns-and-risk.pdf?sfvrsn=b7bc0f65_6)

### 2006-07-12 — You Can’t Eat IRR

An IRR measures returns on the dollars invested when they were invested. A small, quick distribution can produce a high annualized number with little wealth created on committed capital. Debt-funded dividends can boost IRR and leave the company more fragile. Show cash calls, distributions, dollar profit, capital multiple, time, residual value and debt together before ranking investments. [Original memo, pp. 1–13](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2006-07-12-you-cant-eat-irr.pdf?sfvrsn=83bc0f65_6)

### 2006-09-07 — Dare to Be Great

A portfolio close to its peers is unlikely to produce a substantially different result. Outsized weights and unusual assets create an opportunity for both gain and error. Trustees, consultants and committees often discourage such choices through career incentives. Require independent underwriting, assign explicit responsibility for each material position, and decide in advance how long beneficiaries can tolerate divergence. [Original memo, pp. 2–13](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2006-09-07-dare-to-be-great.pdf?sfvrsn=b3bc0f65_6)

### 2006-10-19 — The New Paradigm

Buyouts can profit from a low entry price, operating improvements, financial engineering or a generous sale price. Competition among larger funds removes one or more of those levers even as fixed management fees grow. Underwrite each contributor separately and inspect loss absorption through every debt or structured-credit layer. A deal that needs cheap borrowing and multiple expansion has little room for error. [Original memo, pp. 3–12](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2006-10-19-the-new-paradigm.pdf?sfvrsn=8bbc0f65_6)

### 2006-12-07 — Pigweed

Amaranth’s gas calendar spreads concentrated capital and profits in a position too large to liquidate at quoted prices. Its own purchases may have supported its marks, and collateral needs made timing decisive. Test exit depth against position size, borrowing terms and other investors’ likely responses. A profitable streak can increase the authority of the very trader whose risks deserve review. [Original memo, pp. 2–13](https://www.oaktreecapital.com/docs/default-source/memos/2006-12-07-pigweed.pdf?sfvrsn=bbbc0f65_6)
