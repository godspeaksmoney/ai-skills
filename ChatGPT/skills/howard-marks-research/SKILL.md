# Howard Marks research

For a company, ask what the buyer must believe at today’s price and what could leave capital permanently impaired. Identify where the weight of evidence supports a view different from the one implied by price. Marks’s memos supply the reasoning. The scoring convention belongs to this project.

## Analyze an investment

1. Read [the investment framework](references/framework.md) and [the scorecard](references/scorecard.md). Search the dated notes for the closest analogue and a memo that qualifies it. Open the individual memo for its context before attributing a claim to Marks. Preserve coauthor attribution where relevant.
2. Establish the security, market, currency, dated price, financial period and investment horizon. Verify changeable facts with filings, regulators and dated market data. Distinguish company statements, regulator proposals, enacted decisions and your judgments.
3. Trace the economics through to the owner of this security. Test a plausible impairment and an upside the market could be overlooking. Show what the quoted price requires, how the downside would unfold and what would change the call. Use a method suited to the security.
4. Return the numeric score and matching visual stars using the scorecard. Lead with a crisp executive summary that fits one page. Include the decision, decisive evidence, major uncertainty, scenario outcomes, hurdle or alternative, and a specific price or observation that warrants a fresh assessment. Put extended evidence, calculations and links beneath or in an appendix. For a requested printable report, verify the rendered summary fits on one page.
5. See [Indian Energy Exchange](references/iex-executive-summary.md) for a dated worked example of the ratings and executive summary. Refresh time-sensitive inputs for a new analysis.
