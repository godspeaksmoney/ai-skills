# Research synthesis

Use these techniques to move from a description of the business to a testable claim about the expectations in its price. Paper IDs link through [the archive index](archive-index.md). Historical samples describe their original populations and periods.

## Strategy and competitive advantage

- **Start with the economics of the transaction.** Identify payer, product, repeat behavior, acquisition or distribution costs, fees, and the costs of providing incremental units. In a platform, examine each participant group separately, where the orders clear, how participants switch or use multiple venues, and who can monetize the liquidity. Separate supply-side scale, demand-side network effects, switching costs and regulatory privileges. A platform can create value for the ecosystem while its ability to capture it declines. Papers 012, 017, 029, 040, 062, 066, 101, 128, 133, 134, 143, 160, 167 and 174.
- **Make competitive advantage duration an assumption you can test.** Separate the current ROIC spread, the capital available for investment at a positive spread, and how long new investment remains attractive. In each scenario trace which mechanism decays, the speed of share and fee changes, and the effect on incremental returns. Model the end state rather than extending peak margins into perpetuity. Papers 004, 012, 058, 062, 093, 097, 100, 101, 114, 116, 143, 156, 174 and 180.
- **Map the profit pool and the addressable market.** An industry’s pool is the sum of capital invested times the spread over opportunity cost. See where the pool shifts when standards, rules, distribution or technology change. Estimate adoption, replacement, realistic share and realized price separately. Triangulate the market where feasible, especially when a category boundary changes. Include failed entrants in the reference class. Papers 021, 029, 031, 059, 066, 078, 140, 145, 150, 153, 155 and 165.
- **Value real options when the decision is genuinely contingent.** Identify the economic right, what makes it exclusive or actionable, the trigger, investment to exercise it, time window and payoff. A speculative adjacency may need a milestone and probability before it earns value in the analysis. Ensure option value and core cash flows account for costs once. Papers 042, 128, 155, 166 and 181.

## Accounting and use of capital

- **Reconstruct economics from statements.** Calculate NOPAT on operating earnings. Estimate invested capital consistently from operating assets less operating liabilities or from financing claims after nonoperating assets. Account for leases, acquired goodwill, minority interests, ordinary versus excess cash, customer collateral, and material internally created intangibles. For eligible intangible investment, capitalize and amortize on a reasonable useful life and adjust both sides of ROIC. Where operating capital is near zero, use economic-profit evidence, contribution economics and cash conversion as cross-checks. Papers 022, 023, 027, 028, 030, 033, 036, 091, 135, 161, 164 and 173.
- **Analyze growth in incremental dollars.** Establish a bridge from unit volume and realized price to margins to cash flow. Estimate return on incremental capital over an appropriate investment lag. Separate maintenance from growth expenditures and take account of defensive spend embedded in operating expense. Monitor fixed costs and nonlinearity when demand or pricing changes. Negative current cash flow can be rational when incremental returns exceed the hurdle, and a business returning abundant cash may have limited opportunity to reinvest at its existing ROIC. Papers 016, 027, 030, 035, 036, 045, 068, 110, 168, 181 and 182.
- **Review all capital uses over time.** Compare internal investment, acquisitions, divestitures, debt, cash retention, dividends, repurchases and issuance through value per share. Assess management’s opportunity set at the time, the alternatives abandoned, and the incentive effects. For M&A, bridge synergies and integration costs to the premium. For share transactions, reconcile gross issuance, buybacks, option grants and net dilution and compare price with value. Cash has flexibility value and an opportunity cost. Papers 007, 008, 013, 016, 026, 032, 057, 061, 063, 069, 074, 081, 087, 090, 092, 105, 108, 112, 125, 175 and 176.

## Expectations, valuation and uncertainty

- **Use price as an input.** Describe the steady-state value of existing operations and the value attributable to future opportunity. Reverse a cash-flow model to find one combination of growth, share, margins, reinvestment or competitive advantage duration implied by today's price. Compare that path with a causal thesis and an outside view. A multiple expresses these same assumptions and can obscure differences in capex, taxes, financing, subsidiaries, and capital returns. Papers 002, 004, 015, 019, 038, 041, 043, 045, 051, 095, 104, 123, 132, 151, 152, 154, 157, 170, 177 and 180.
- **Apply opportunity cost consistently.** Match the currency and nominal or real convention, discount free cash flow to the appropriate claimants, reconcile operating value to equity value and stress discount rate against terminal growth. Determine whether terminal margins and reinvestment imply a feasible marginal return. Distinguish price volatility, a forced exit and permanently impaired cash flows. Papers 025, 042, 095, 099, 113, 121, 123, 149 and 177.
- **Use base rates as priors.** Choose a reference class matched to what is being predicted. Starting size, industry mix, inflation, sample selection and time horizon matter. Include exits and failed firms where the question calls for them. Shrink toward an empirically defensible mean at a speed consistent with persistence. Update with diagnostic business evidence and consider how changes in technology or rules affect comparability. Where credible data are sparse, label estimates as judgments. Papers 003, 005, 009, 018, 021, 039, 064, 070–072, 075, 077, 083, 086, 094, 100, 103, 109, 116, 138 and 145.
- **Separate odds, payoffs and confidence.** Use disjoint scenarios that span meaningful states of the world and probability-weight equity values. Include a realistic severe downside when returns, liquidity or participant behavior can change nonlinearly. Maintain a dated forecast and observations that would move its probabilities. Quality of evidence, confidence in the estimates and position sizing answer different questions. Papers 009, 010, 024, 048, 052, 073, 077, 080, 082, 086, 098, 115, 118, 124, 142, 146 and 148.

## Markets, institutions and decision process

- **Explain who is on the other side and why a gap persists.** Evaluate behavioral, analytical, informational and technical explanations. Cost and friction matter for implementation. Market prices can aggregate dispersed information when cognition is diverse, bids are aggregated effectively and incentives align. Common signals, reflexivity and leverage can change the behavior of the whole market. Papers 001, 006, 009, 011, 014, 046, 047, 049, 050, 052–054, 060, 084, 085, 102, 106, 115, 119, 126, 129–131, 144, 158, 169, 171 and 178.
- **Measure processes with meaningful feedback.** Use metrics that are persistent and predict the outcome of interest. Record the information available at decision time, ask for independent estimates, solicit disconfirming evidence and review resolved forecasts. Match team structure, incentives and evaluation period to the source of edge. Papers 018, 024, 034, 037, 048, 055, 065, 067, 071, 076, 077, 079, 082, 088, 089, 094, 096, 098, 102, 107–109, 111, 117, 120, 122, 127, 136, 137, 139, 141, 147, 163, 172 and 179.
- **Mind the benchmark and the changing universe.** Decompose public-market concentration, index entry and exit, skewed wealth creation and cash-flow timing. Compare private investments to an investable public alternative on matched cash flows and suitable risk, vintage, leverage and liquidity. A strong IRR on early distributions can coexist with a weak total multiple. Papers 011, 014, 019–021, 044, 056, 058, 060, 093, 107, 169 and 183.

## Edition history to consider

- **Moat and duration.** Paper 180 introduces the CAP framework, 143, 101, 062 and 012 carry successive moat frameworks, and 004 updates the valuation and fade treatment. Keep the year when using base rates or exhibits.
- **Capital allocation.** Compare the 2014, 2015, 2016, 2022 and 2025 U.S. releases (090, 081, 063, 026, 007) with non-U.S. reports 087 and 061. Paper 013 looks specifically at simultaneous issuance and retirement.
- **Forecasting.** The Base Rate Book (064) and earlier stand-alone chapters (070, 072, 075, 083) have different dating and scope. Papers 005 and 003 show how a recent view can be revised and how matching a reference class changes interpretation.
- **Public and private equity.** Keep the 2020 and 2026 reports (044 and 183) separate, especially when judging interest rates, exits and headline returns.
- **Customer economics and market efficiency.** Compare 133 with the more detailed 040 cohort valuation and 050 with 006's expanded treatment of market efficiency. Pick the edition that supplies the actual claimed method or evidence.
