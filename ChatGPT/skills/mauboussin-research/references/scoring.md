# Business and valuation scoring rubric

This rubric is a local implementation informed by the research. The weights, score anchors, star bands and scenario probabilities are analyst choices.

| Dimension | Weight | Evidence to score |
|---|---:|---|
| Current economics | 20 | Adjusted ROIC or the appropriate sector equivalent against the same-currency opportunity cost. Include normalized cash conversion, unit economics, leases, intangible investment and recurring stock compensation. Disclose when invested capital is too small or uncertain for a useful ROIC point estimate. |
| Durability of advantage | 25 | Demand and supply mechanisms, customer and supplier power, substitutes, network effects and the competitive advantage period. Model how returns fade under entry, technology changes, regulation and customer switching. |
| Profitable reinvestment | 15 | Returns on incremental capital, addressable market, realistic share and pricing, maintenance needs and capital required. Value unproved adjacencies as contingent opportunities with costs and milestones. |
| Capital allocation and governance | 10 | History of reinvestment, M&A, issuance, repurchases and dividends. Assess value per share, execution, incentives and minority-owner protection. |
| Financial resilience | 10 | Liquidity, restrictions and client money, working-capital or collateral swings, leverage, covenants and funding access under stress. |
| Expectations and valuation | 20 | Market-implied performance versus an outside view and operating evidence, probability-weighted value relative to price and downside asymmetry. |

Score each dimension 0–5 in 0.5 increments. Anchor 0 at materially adverse, 1 at weak, 2 at below average, 3 at adequate or mixed-positive, 4 at strong and 5 at exceptional with sustained supporting evidence. Use intermediate marks when the evidence straddles anchors. Show an observation, a countervailing factor and the reason for the grade. Confidence is a separate judgment. Identify genuinely missing facts and show a range when plausible ratings would move the headline score.

For durability, grade near 2 when a key economic advantage faces a credible structural break and retention is unproved, 3 when several safeguards can preserve part of the advantage, and 4 or more when independent mechanisms have defended economics through actual entry or disruption. For reinvestment, grade near 2 when incremental spreads, repeatable opportunities or runway are weakly evidenced, 3 when there is demonstrated profitable growth with material uncertainty about future economics, and 4 or more when incremental returns and room to deploy are both substantiated. Scale these anchors to maturity and business type.

For valuation, use the expected value gap (probability-weighted intrinsic value / observed price − 1) as a first anchor. A gap below −35% maps to 0, −35% to below −15% to 1, −15% to below −5% to 2, −5% to below +10% to 3, +10% to below +25% to 4, and +25% or greater to 5. If downside shape or evidential weakness warrants moving the mark, disclose why and by how much. Avoid marking the same risk repeatedly for the same reason. Durability grades the operating mechanism and valuation grades what the price pays for those outcomes.

Compute each contribution as weight × grade / 5. Sum before rounding, round half up to a whole number and floor at 1. Convert the displayed score to stars using equal bands.

## Modeling discipline

- Estimate the return on incremental invested capital and competitive advantage period from economics. Include a reference class matched to business model, geography, scale and starting conditions where feasible. Explain transfer limits and distinguish sample frequencies from judgment-based probabilities.
- Link volume, realized price, margin, reinvestment and cash flows. For FCFF, discount at WACC and bridge enterprise value to equity. For FCFE, discount at cost of equity and use a consistent lease and financing treatment. Match nominal or real units and currency. Apply a terminal growth rate below the discount rate and explain the compatible reinvestment and returns.
- Check the valuation when one or two material assumptions change. Solve for a specific implied path at the observed price, holding other assumptions explicit. Multiple paths can generate the same price. State probabilities, scenario values and what would lead to an update.
- When a plausible change to a grade moves the score across a star threshold, report the band of plausible scores and stars alongside the central rating.
- Use sector-appropriate equivalents for banks, insurers, commodity producers, early-stage and structurally capital-light businesses. If price is required and unavailable, give a provisional score or range and explain the valuation evidence gap.
