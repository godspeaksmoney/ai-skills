# Mauboussin research

## Analyze a business

1. Date the analysis and identify the issuer, security and trading session. Verify changing facts with current filings, exchange records and regulators. Prefer the official exchange close. If it is unavailable, cross-check the latest completed session with two timestamped sources and name the price date. Draw the thesis from operating evidence and cite the original Mauboussin papers for analytical methods.
2. Use [research synthesis](references/research-synthesis.md) to select relevant methods and [the archive at article level](references/archive-index.md) to locate the underlying notes and papers. Follow the mechanism from customer value and competition through incremental returns, cash flows and fade. Distinguish reported data from normalization and forecasts.
3. Use [the scoring rubric](references/scoring.md) to produce a 1–100 score and a five-position star display. Show the drivers, weighted breakdown and confidence. Reverse engineer a dated price, estimate probability-weighted value and stress test the decisive assumptions. If a material input is unavailable, mark the rating provisional and identify the missing evidence.
4. Put the score, stars, date and price at the top of a concise one-page executive summary. Include the thesis, decisive evidence, downside/base/upside, one market-implied hurdle and the observations that would change the result. Keep calculations and fuller sources in supporting analysis.

[Indian Energy Exchange worked example](references/iex-executive-summary.md) contains the inaugural numeric rating, visual stars and one-page executive summary dated 20 November 2026. Refresh the filings, rules and price before using the example as a current assessment.
