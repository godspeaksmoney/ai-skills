# Berkshire Hathaway shareholder letters, 1977–2025

This is a research index with one decision-relevant note for every requested letter. Years are Berkshire’s reporting years. Warren Buffett authored the 1977–2024 main letters. Greg Abel authored the 2025 letter, and the 2014 anniversary material includes Charlie Munger's retrospective.

The archive pass covered 21 official HTML letters and 28 official PDFs (571 PDF pages). All 49 source files were acquired and text-extracted, screened across the corpus for the investment themes, and reviewed year by year through headings, selected passages and the principal case studies. The scanned 2010 PDF page 22 was read visually. The notes synthesize investing and business-analysis material and serve as pointers for close reading of the original sources. [Berkshire archive](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/letters.html) · [2025 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf)

## Annual notes

### 1977

**Return on beginning equity; Textile Operations; Insurance Investments.** Compare earnings gains with capital added and adjust for unusual leverage or carrying values. National Indemnity and textiles show how industry economics can produce very different outcomes for capable operators. The listed-equity selection tests combine understandable business, prospects, people and price. [Official 1977 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1977.html)

### 1978

**Insurance Investments; earnings by business.** Consolidated figures can obscure unlike segment economics. SAFECO illustrates the value of management's use of investee retained earnings. The contrast between quoted minority stakes and negotiated control deals makes acquisition opportunity cost explicit. [Official 1978 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1978.html)

### 1979

**Long Term Results; Insurance Investments; Financial Reporting.** Judge unlevered operating returns and real after-tax compounding. The long-bond experience shows how stated carrying values can conceal economic loss and mismatch against inflation-sensitive claims. [Official 1979 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1979.html)

### 1980

**Non-Controlled Ownership Earnings; Insurance Industry Conditions.** Book dividends understate the economics of productive reinvestment. GEICO illustrates how to distinguish a damaged franchise that can recover from a structurally weak business. Inspect marked assets, obligations and underwriting economics together. [Official 1980 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1980.html)

### 1981

**General Acquisition Behavior; Equity Value-Added.** An inflation-resistant business can raise prices with modest volume loss and accommodate growth without absorbing proportionate capital. Acquisition premiums and personal taxes can absorb returns that look attractive on an accounting basis. [Official 1981 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1981.html)

### 1982

**Insurance Industry Conditions; stock-for-stock acquisition discussion.** Excess capacity plus undifferentiated output compresses returns. GEICO's cost position is a credible exception. Value stock issued in acquisitions at intrinsic value and compare it with what owners receive. [Official 1982 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1982.html)

### 1983

**Owner principles; Nebraska Furniture Mart; Goodwill appendix.** Owner alignment, conservative debt, and per-share intrinsic value guide the partnership. See's shows economic goodwill through earnings far above the required return on tangible capital. Separate accounting amortization from real franchise deterioration. [Official 1983 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1983.html)

### 1984

**Washington Public Power Supply System; Dividend Policy.** Think in probabilities and terms as well as asset labels. Retain earnings where incremental deployment is likely to create value above the amount retained. Repurchases help continuing owners when executed materially below intrinsic value with capacity to fund them. [Official 1984 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1984.html)

### 1985

**Textile Operations; managerial compensation; Washington Post.** Textile investment that looks attractive in isolation can fail once competitors match it. Reward real economic improvement and capital discipline. Purchased positions benefit from favorable price, underlying economics and owner-minded management. [Official 1985 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1985.html)

### 1986

**Scott Fetzer; Purchase-Price Accounting Adjustments and the Cash Flow Fallacy.** Reconcile reported profit to owner earnings with genuine noncash charges, necessary maintenance spending and incremental working capital. Use prevailing competitive and volume requirements to estimate maintenance. Compare acquisitions with debt reduction and waiting. [Official 1986 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1986.html)

### 1987

**Marketable Securities – Permanent Holdings; capital allocation.** Returns on a business's own capital and returns on the price the investor pays are different calculations. Permanent holdings require continuing economic prospects and trusted management. A chief executive's repeated reinvestment decisions can reshape the company. [Official 1987 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1987.html)

### 1988

**Marketable Securities; Efficient Market Theory.** The Arcata arbitrage example distinguishes contingent outcomes from ordinary franchise ownership. Ask what must happen, when, and what downside exists. A competitive market can still offer occasional price-value gaps. [Official 1988 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1988.html)

### 1989

**Zero-Coupon Securities; Mistakes of the First Twenty-Five Years.** Accrued interest and maintenance spending matter to debt capacity even when current cash coupons or promoted metrics omit them. Buffett revisits the textile purchase, structural economics, the institutional imperative, and the opportunity cost of cheap but mediocre businesses. [Official 1989 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1989.html)

### 1990

**Measuring Insurance Performance; Marketable Securities; Appendices A and B.** Apply cost-of-float accounting over time and test correlated loss exposure. The bank example makes management quality critical under leverage. Media earnings show how a formerly strong franchise can lose value when customer alternatives expand. [Official 1990 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1990.html)

### 1991

**Look-Through Earnings; media franchise; See’s; Mistake Du Jour.** A franchise supplies a wanted product with limited close substitutes and defensible pricing. See's links incremental after-tax earnings to very little additional capital. Evaluate each investee's contribution to long-run look-through earnings. [Official 1991 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1991.html)

### 1992

**The Salomon Interlude; Common Stock Investments; accounting discussion.** Growth creates value when incremental returns justify the capital consumed. Value investing integrates business economics and price. Salomon illustrates the cost of trust failures and the importance of corrective action. Stock options have an economic cost. [Official 1992 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1992.html)

### 1993

**Dexter Shoe; Common Stock Investments; Corporate Governance.** Evaluate the risk of poor long-run owner receipts using predictability, management, price, inflation and tax. Examine who can hold management accountable. Dexter is an instructive original thesis to read beside the explicit 2007 reversal. [Official 1993 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1993.html)

### 1994

**Book Value and Intrinsic Value; Mistake Du Jour.** Intrinsic value is the discounted worth of cash distributable over a business's remaining life. Capital allocation and compensation should reward per-share value creation. The USAir preferred investment shows how protective terms can coexist with weak industry economics. [Official 1994 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1994.html)

### 1995

**GEICO; A Proposed Recapitalization.** GEICO's distribution-cost advantage provides value to customers and supports its moat. Compare whole-company deals with minority investments. Assess transaction terms, incentives and effect on existing holders, including a new share class. [Official 1995 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1995.html)

### 1996

**Insurance Operations; Common Stock Investments; Class B offering.** GEICO's cost gap and quantified underwriting incentives illustrate how management can widen advantage. Seek understandable businesses with durable earnings power at a sensible price. Draw a boundary around what can be appraised with confidence. [Official 1996 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1996.html)

### 1997

**How We Think About Market Fluctuations; A Confession; Look-Through Earnings.** For a continuing buyer, price declines can benefit new purchases and investee repurchases. Accounting and contractual terms can disguise real compensation costs. Measure GEICO's acquisition spending in relation to longer-run economics, float and customer growth. [Official 1997 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1997.html)

### 1998

**General Re; GEICO customer acquisition; unconventional holdings.** General Re expands float and brings liabilities that need to be understood. GEICO's first-year customer-acquisition costs illustrate how a near-term earnings sacrifice can be rational when customer economics and retention support it. Compare the acquisition currency with value obtained. [Official 1998 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1998pdf.pdf)

### 1999

**Acquisition Accounting; Share Repurchases; GEICO.** Buffett owns the weak relative result and explains divergent operating performance. Buybacks need surplus resources and sufficient evidence that price is below intrinsic value. Economic goodwill changes independently of accounting charges. [Official 1999 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/final1999pdf.pdf)

### 2000

**Acquisitions; insurance operations; Look-Through Earnings.** Examine acquisition price, operating fit and the people running the asset instead of requiring internal synergies. GEICO's durable direct-distribution cost advantage matters over a cycle. Distinguish underwriting decisions from how their occasional losses appear in quarterly results. [Official 2000 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2000pdf.pdf)

### 2001

**Insurance operations; September 11; underwriting rules.** Price exposures, limit aggregation and preserve capacity after extreme loss. General Re's terrorism exposure illustrates how unwritten assumptions about correlation can defeat an otherwise credible underwriting record. [Official 2001 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2001pdf.pdf)

### 2002

**Insurance Operations; derivatives; Corporate Governance; The Audit Committee.** Mark-to-model incentives, counterparty chains and concentrated exposure can hide leveraged risk. Audit committees should ask pointed questions of auditors. Treat employee options as compensation and discipline insurance reserves against past estimation errors. [Official 2002 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2002pdf.pdf)

### 2003

**Clayton Homes; Corporate Governance; Regulated Utility Businesses.** Clayton shows how industry underwriting and financing experience can inform acquisition judgment. Trace returns on loans retained versus income recognized on sale. Directors' incentives and ownership should align them with outside owners. [Official 2003 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2003ltr.pdf)

### 2004

**Sector Results; General Re Securities wind-down.** Use sector-level results with business-specific denominators. The derivative run-off demonstrates that realizable value and ease of exit can differ substantially from booked marks. Test underwriting through reserve development and long settlement periods. [Official 2004 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2004ltr.pdf)

### 2005

**Insurance; Katrina; Regulated Utility Business; Gen Re derivatives.** Catastrophe frequency, severity and clustering can change. Quantify retained loss capacity. PacifiCorp illustrates an avenue for large capital deployment under a regulatory framework. Run-off losses are a check on prior marks and incentives. [Official 2005 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2005ltr.pdf)

### 2006

**Yardsticks; acquisitions and bolt-ons; Clayton.** Compare investments per share with operating earnings separately when valuing a mixed holding company. Track bolt-on purchases and judge financing advantages alongside local operational skill. At scale, opportunity size affects how much even good deals can matter. [Official 2006 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2006ltr.pdf)

### 2007

**Businesses – The Great, the Good and the Gruesome; Dexter Shoe.** See's, FlightSafety and airlines span a spectrum of returns and reinvestment needs. A high-return business can create value while distributing cash it cannot compound internally. Dexter shows that a moat can disappear and expensive equity issuance magnifies the loss. [Official 2007 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2007ltr.pdf)

### 2008

**Insurance; regulated utilities; crisis financing; major mistakes.** Preserve liquidity and avoid relying on short-term funding when markets close. Re-examine decisions such as ConocoPhillips through purchase price and normalized commodity economics. Clayton's underwriting emphasizes real affordability and customer equity. [Official 2008 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2008ltr.pdf)

### 2009

**How We Measure Ourselves; BNSF; An Inconvenient Truth.** A railroad can be an attractive home for a large amount of capital even with heavy reinvestment. Translate cash and stock consideration into owner value. Judge reporting and balance-sheet conservatism when financing is scarce. [Official 2009 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2009ltr.pdf)

### 2010

**Intrinsic value components; regulated capital-intensive businesses; liquidity appendices.** Combine investments, operating earnings and the future deployment of retained earnings. A utility's social compact provides an opportunity to reinvest under conditions set by regulators. The scanned Ernest Buffett letter on PDF p. 22 makes the case for reserves usable on short notice. [Official 2010 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2010ltr.pdf)

### 2011

**Share Repurchases; regulated businesses; productive assets.** Repurchases require suitable liquidity and a sensible valuation, illustrated by Berkshire's then-current limits and the IBM example. Powerful productive assets can expand real output with relatively little incremental capital. A regulated operator's customer relationship supports its permission to earn. [Official 2011 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2011ltr.pdf)

### 2012

**Intrinsic Business Value; Dividend Policy; liquidity.** Compare operating reinvestment, acquisitions, discounted repurchases and shareholder distributions. A payout decision should preserve owner value, including the ability to sell a small portion of shares where assumptions hold. Keep genuinely redundant layers of liquidity. [Official 2012 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2012ltr.pdf)

### 2013

**Some Thoughts About Investing; NV Energy; intrinsic value.** The farm and New York property examples begin with sustainable output, costs and price. Ignore quotation noise when an asset's productive economics are intelligible. Use low-cost broad exposure when individual appraisal is outside the investor's capability. [Official 2013 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2013ltr.pdf)

### 2014

**Berkshire – Past, Present and Future; Munger’s anniversary essay.** The 50-year retrospectives trace the move from statistically cheap assets toward excellent businesses at defensible prices. Berkshire's structure, cash, culture and decentralized succession matter to replicating results. Issuing Berkshire shares for Dexter converted an operating mistake into a much larger opportunity cost. [Official 2014 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2014ltr.pdf)

### 2015

**Kraft Heinz; Precision Castparts; Important Risks.** Distinguish ownership accounting and net-income effects from operating performance after transactions. Test whether large acquisitions put funds to work at durable returns. Evaluate industry-specific risks even for a diversified owner, including regulated operations and insurance. [Official 2015 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2015ltr.pdf)

### 2016

**Share Repurchases; operating earnings; investment-fee wager.** Buyback attractiveness depends on price relative to conservatively estimated value. Ignore per-share accounting improvements from expensive buybacks. The index-versus-funds-of-funds comparison isolates the long-run drag of layered fees. [Official 2016 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2016ltr.pdf)

### 2017

**Acquisitions; GAAP earnings; The Bet.** Cheap financing can make expensive acquisitions look EPS-accretive. Evaluate their economics on an all-equity basis where appropriate. Large unrealized holdings swings can overwhelm reported profit. A liquid investor can respond to market declines without using forced leverage. [Official 2017 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2017ltr.pdf)

### 2018

**Focus on the Forest – Forget the Trees; book value; The American Tailwind.** Book value loses tracking power when operating assets are held at stale carrying values and value-accretive buybacks reduce book value per share. Build a holding-company bridge across operating and investment assets, insurance, and obligations. [Official 2018 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2018ltr.pdf)

### 2019

**The Power of Retained Earnings; Berkshire Hathaway Energy; The Road Ahead.** Reinvested profits matter across controlled businesses and equity holdings. BHE's retained capital supports sustained regulated deployment. Focus on sustainable returns after depreciation, interest, compensation and actual costs. Succession and board independence underpin the long view. [Official 2019 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2019ltr.pdf)

### 2020

**Berkshire’s largest assets; repurchases; The Berkshire Partnership.** Apple's share repurchases increased Berkshire's ownership while Berkshire's own below-value buybacks raised continuing owners' indirect interest. Distinguish the economics of customers, employees, creditors and owners. Compare fixed-income alternatives with productive assets. [Official 2020 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2020ltr.pdf)

### 2021

**What You Own; Our Four Giants; Share Repurchases.** Understand what lies beneath the listed holding, including insurance float, Apple, BNSF and BHE. Test incremental underlying cash flows and repurchase prices. Patience is an allocation decision when otherwise attractive opportunities are overpriced. [Official 2021 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2021ltr.pdf)

### 2022

**What We Do; The Secret Sauce.** Coca-Cola and American Express show decades of growth in distributions and value. A few exceptional decisions can dominate compound returns, and ordinary mistakes still deserve scrutiny. Compare operating earnings with quoted-price-driven GAAP swings. [Official 2022 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2022ltr.pdf)

### 2023

**What We Do; Our Not-So-Secret Weapon; BNSF and BHE.** Concentrate on durable enterprises, avoid permanent capital loss, and hold liquidity when the opportunity set is thin. Buffett explicitly revises BHE's risk assessment as wildfire liabilities and regulatory economics challenge the historic utility compact. [Official 2023 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2023ltr.pdf)

### 2024

**Mistakes – Yes, We Make Them; Where Your Money Is; insurance.** Recognize and correct mistakes promptly. Long-run reinvestment and decentralized operating returns matter more than one year's mark-to-market gains. Insurance pricing quality is only evident after claims develop. Buffett previews Abel as the future author. [Official 2024 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2024ltr.pdf)

### 2025

**Our Culture; Capital Discipline; Risk Management; segment operations.** Greg Abel reaffirms per-share intrinsic value, operating autonomy with accountability, liquidity and value-sensitive buybacks. He applies the principles to BNSF operating improvement, BHE wildfire exposure and regulated investment, GEICO segmentation, and prioritizing underwriting profit over volume. [Official 2025 letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/2025ltr.pdf)
